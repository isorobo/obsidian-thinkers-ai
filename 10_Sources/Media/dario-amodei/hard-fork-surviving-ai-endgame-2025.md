# Anthropic's CEO Dario Amodei on Surviving the A.I. Endgame

## Citation

Amodei, Dario, interviewed by Kevin Roose and Casey Newton. "Anthropic's C.E.O. Dario Amodei on Surviving the A.I. Endgame". Hard Fork, The New York Times, 28 February 2025. https://podscripts.co/podcasts/hard-fork/anthropics-ceo-dario-amodei-on-surviving-the-ai-endgame.

## One-line summary

Amodei discusses the Claude 3.7 Sonnet release, concedes a naming misstep, and warns that background AI risk is rising even as public attention to risk recedes.

## Key claims

- The Claude 3.7 Sonnet release was designed around two main objectives.
- The "3.7" naming was a misstep, made hard to correct by API-versioning constraints.
- Risk in the background has increased even as the public mood worries less about it.
- A workable plan exists for not racing at maximum speed, but it depends on specific conditions.
- The arrival of highly capable AI is coming and will shock people when it lands.

## Excerpts

> "So we've been working on this model for a while. We basically had in mind two things."
> ~ Hard Fork, 28 February 2025

> "I recognize the 3.7 new was a misstep. It actually turns out to be hard to change the name in the API."
> ~ Hard Fork, 28 February 2025

> "I think contrary to the environment that we're seeing today of worrying less about the risks, the risks in the background have actually been increasing."
> ~ Hard Fork, 28 February 2025

> "I think it's going to happen. I'm just worried that it'll be a shock to people when it happens."
> ~ Hard Fork, 28 February 2025

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Amodei treats a product-naming admission and a civilisational-risk warning in the same register, reflecting an engineer's comfort with admitting error and a strategist's focus on stakes.
- The observation that "risks in the background have actually been increasing" while public concern falls foreshadows the inversion he later names in The Adolescence of Technology.
- His framing of a plan that depends on conditions, rather than a guaranteed safe path, matches his rejection of "aligned by default" optimism.
