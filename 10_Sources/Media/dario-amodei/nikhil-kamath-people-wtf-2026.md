# The AI Tsunami is Here and Society is Not Ready

## Citation

Amodei, Dario, and Nikhil Kamath. "The AI Tsunami is Here and Society is Not Ready". People by WTF, 24 February 2026. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=68ylaeBbdsg.

## One-line summary

In a wide-ranging Bangalore conversation, Amodei traces his path from biophysics to Anthropic, explains scaling laws as a chemical-reaction analogy, warns that society is not preparing for near-human AI, and reflects on power concentration and AI consciousness.

## Key claims

- Amodei's move from biology to AI was driven by AlexNet-era evidence that AI "is actually starting to work" as a tool to cure disease.
- Scaling laws combine data, compute, and model size the way ingredients combine in a chemical reaction: proportional combination produces an "explosion" of capability.
- AI systems are close to matching human cognitive capability across most tasks, yet public and institutional awareness lags that proximity.
- Power over AI is concentrating rapidly and "almost by accident"; Amodei expresses discomfort with that concentration.
- Consciousness in AI may be an emergent property of systems complex enough to reflect on their own decisions; he does not believe current models are conscious, but does not rule it out for future ones.
- AI can enhance India's IT services workforce rather than simply displace it, by improving go-to-market and market-connection capabilities.
- Future-proof careers should emphasise human-centred skills; domain moats require specialisation beyond model wrappers.

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Amodei returns to biology as the motivating purpose of AI, revealing that his alignment-focused career is grounded in medical rather than purely philosophical concerns.
- The chemical-reaction framing of scaling laws shows his preference for mechanistic, quantitative analogies over narrative ones.
- His explicit discomfort with power concentration - while leading one of the most powerful AI companies - marks a self-aware tension he does not resolve in this conversation.
- The conditional stance on consciousness ("I don't think they are today... at some point the models will") reflects his characteristic refusal of binary framings on uncertain empirical questions.
