# Prepared Remarks on Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy

## Citation

Amodei, Dario. "Dario Amodei's Prepared Remarks from the AI Safety Summit on Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy". Anthropic, 1 November 2023. https://www.anthropic.com/news/uk-ai-safety-summit.

## One-line summary

Amodei presents the Responsible Scaling Policy as a formal, board-directed protocol of AI Safety Levels, framing it as a working prototype for the regulation he expects to follow.

## Key claims

- AI capabilities advance unpredictably; the timing of specific skills or knowledge is hard to forecast.
- The RSP defines AI Safety Levels (ASL), modelled on the biosafety-level (BSL) system.
- Each level pairs capability thresholds with safety, security, and testing requirements before further scaling.
- The policy is a formal board directive, accountable through Anthropic's Long Term Benefit Trust.
- RSPs are a prototype for regulation, not a substitute for it.
- Executive ownership is explicit; Amodei reports spending substantial personal time drafting the policy.

## Excerpts

> "it is actually very difficult to predict when AI will acquire specific skills or knowledge"
> ~ On AI's unpredictability

> "we've come up with a system called AI safety levels (ASL), loosely modeled after the internationally recognized BSL system"
> ~ On the RSP framework

> "As CEO, I personally spent 10-20% of my time on the RSP for 3 months—I wrote multiple drafts from scratch"
> ~ On executive commitment

> "RSPs are not intended as a substitute for regulation, but rather a prototype for it"
> ~ On RSP's purpose relative to regulation

> "Anthropic's RSP is a formal directive of its board, which ultimately is accountable to our Long Term Benefit Trust"
> ~ On accountability mechanisms

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Borrowing the BSL analogy shows Amodei's preference for importing tested governance structures rather than inventing safety regimes from scratch.
- Calling the RSP "a prototype for regulation" confirms his view that voluntary commitments are a floor that should harden into law.
- The emphasis on board accountability and personal drafting time signals that he treats safety governance as an executive duty, not a compliance afterthought.
