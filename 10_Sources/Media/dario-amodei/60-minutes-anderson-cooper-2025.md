# Dario Amodei on 60 Minutes with Anderson Cooper

## Citation

Amodei, Dario, interviewed by Anderson Cooper. "Why Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei Spends So Much Time Warning of AI's Potential Dangers". 60 Minutes, CBS News, 16 November 2025. https://www.cbsnews.com/news/anthropic-ceo-dario-amodei-warning-of-ai-potential-dangers-60-minutes-transcript/.

## One-line summary

In a broadcast interview Amodei restates that AI will surpass human capability across most domains, warns of entry-level white-collar job loss, and argues that a few companies should not set AI policy alone.

## Key claims

- AI will reach a level smarter than most or all humans in most or all ways.
- The unknowns dominate his worry; outcomes cannot be predicted with certainty.
- Entry-level white-collar roles in consulting, law, and finance are already within reach of current models.
- No democratic mandate authorises a handful of company leaders to decide AI's trajectory, which is why he advocates regulation.
- Public warning is part of his role rather than a contradiction of building the technology.

## Excerpts

> "I, I believe it will reach that level, that it will be smarter than most or all humans in most or all ways."
> ~ 60 Minutes, 16 November 2025

> "I worry a lot about the unknowns. I don't think we can predict everything for sure."
> ~ 60 Minutes, 16 November 2025

> "Well, if we look at entry-level consultants, lawyers, financial professionals, you know, many of, kind of the white-collar service industries, a lot of what they do, you know, AI models are already quite good at."
> ~ 60 Minutes, 16 November 2025

> "No one, no one. Honestly, no one. And, and this is one reason why I've always advocated for responsible and thoughtful regulation of the technology."
> ~ 60 Minutes, 16 November 2025 (answering "Who elected you and Sam Altman?")

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- The interview shows Amodei carrying his written-essay positions into mass media without softening the labour warning, matching the 2025 shift the positions file tracks.
- His answer "No one" to the question of mandate reveals discomfort with self-regulation by labs and reinforces his call for external rules.
- Naming specific professions rather than abstract "jobs" continues his preference for concrete, testable claims.
