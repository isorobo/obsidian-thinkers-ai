# Erik Brynjolfsson on How AI Is Rewriting the Rules of the Economy

## Citation

Brynjolfsson, Erik, and Bill Kerr. "Erik Brynjolfsson on How AI Is Rewriting the Rules of the Economy". Managing the Future of Work podcast, Harvard Business School, 26 March 2025. https://www.hbs.edu/managing-the-future-of-work/podcast/Pages/podcast-details.aspx?episode=9166436185.

## One-line summary

Brynjolfsson explains his task-based view of AI's labour impact, his optimism about a productivity take-off, and his maxim that AI-using managers will replace those who do not.

## Key claims

- AI tools function like a capable graduate student that turbocharges productivity without always being right.
- Managers who use AI will replace managers who do not.
- Jobs consist of dozens of distinct tasks, so AI's impact must be analysed task by task.
- Productivity is likely to take off over the next five years or so.

## Excerpts

> "I would compare it to a good grad student, that they don't necessarily have all the answers, and they don't always get everything right, but totally turbocharges my productivity."
> ~ Managing the Future of Work, 26 March 2025

> "AI will not replace managers, but managers who use AI will replace managers who don't."
> ~ Managing the Future of Work, 26 March 2025

> "Most jobs consist of dozens of specific tasks-even call centers, radiologists, economists, professors, truck drivers. There's many different individual tasks they do."
> ~ Managing the Future of Work, 26 March 2025

> "I'm pretty optimistic about productivity taking off over the next five years or so."
> ~ Managing the Future of Work, 26 March 2025

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- The task-based framing restates the method behind his 2017 Science work with Tom Mitchell on what machine learning can do.
- His five-year productivity forecast places the present at the upswing of the J-curve.
- The grad-student analogy shows his preference for augmentation framing even in casual description.
