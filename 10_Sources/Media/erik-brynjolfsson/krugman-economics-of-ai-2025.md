# How Should We Think About the Economics of AI? (2025)

## Citation

Krugman, P. "How Should We Think About the Economics of AI?" (conversation with Erik Brynjolfsson). Paul Krugman Substack, 22 March 2025 (recorded 20 March 2025). https://paulkrugman.substack.com/p/how-should-we-think-about-the-economics.

## One-line summary

Brynjolfsson tells Krugman that AI is a general purpose technology, that official statistics miss much digital value, and that the open question is shared prosperity.

## Key claims

- AI belongs with steam and electricity as a general purpose technology.
- Free digital goods are missing from productivity statistics.
- Early adoption involves reskilling costs that depress measured productivity before gains appear.
- The central policy question is how to share prosperity.

## Excerpts

> "General Purpose Technologies are things like the steam engine, electricity, and I think AI."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, about 7:30

> "we're missing a lot of the digital revolution just by design. Zoom or Wikipedia or search, most Facebook value for end users, they aren't counted in the productivity statistics."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, about 17:00

> "at first you have to reskill your workforce, put a lot of time into training and people on their own have to figure things out."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, about 21:00

> "how do we figure out a system for not just having prosperity, but for having shared prosperity?"
> ~ Brynjolfsson, about 48:30

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- He reaches for historical analogy (steam, electricity) to place AI, which reads as a preference for long-run technology patterns.
- He ends on distribution, which suggests prosperity is the harder problem in his view.
