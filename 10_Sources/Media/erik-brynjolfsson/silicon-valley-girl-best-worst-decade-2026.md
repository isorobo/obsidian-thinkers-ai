# Stanford Economist on the Best and Worst Decade in History (2026)

## Citation

Mogilko, M. "Stanford Economist on the Best and Worst Decade in History" (interview with Erik Brynjolfsson). Silicon Valley Girl Podcast, 21 July 2026. https://marinamogilko.co/episode/72duHF7iZiU.

## One-line summary

Brynjolfsson expects millions of jobs to disappear, most workers to manage fleets of agents, and the coming decade to be the best in history if choices go well.

## Key claims

- Millions of jobs will disappear, with coding, call centres, sales and marketing most exposed.
- Demand response to falling prices varies in steepness and matters a lot.
- Most people's jobs will involve managing a fleet of agents.
- A good outcome depends on choices.

## Excerpts

> "There are a bunch of jobs, millions of jobs that are going to disappear."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, 00:49

> "Coding is obviously dead center. Call centers, parts of sales, marketing."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, 02:03

> "If you cut the price, more people can buy. But the steepness varies a lot, it matters a lot."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, 04:27

> "Most people, their job will be managing agents, not just one agent, but like a whole fleet of agents."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, 12:47

> "The next decade, if we play our cards right, will be the best decade in human history by far."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, 50:46

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- He pairs blunt displacement claims with a strongly optimistic ending, which reads as conditional optimism ("if we play our cards right").
- Using price elasticity to explain labour demand suggests an economist's reflex to reason from demand curves.
