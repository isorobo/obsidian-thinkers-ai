# Q&A - AI and the Future of Work with Erik Brynjolfsson and Tom Mitchell (2024)

## Citation

Smith, M. "Q&A: AI and the Future of Work with Erik Brynjolfsson and Tom Mitchell" (interview with Erik Brynjolfsson). Stanford Digital Economy Lab, 22 November 2024. https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/qa-ai-and-the-future-of-work-with-erik-brynjolfsson-and-tom-mitchell/.

## One-line summary

Brynjolfsson discusses a workforce report begun before ChatGPT and concludes that the future is not predetermined and that choices grow more consequential as tools improve.

## Key claims

- The report was begun in 2022, before ChatGPT's release.
- Top experts from academia and industry were assembled to assess workforce implications.
- LLMs enable uses such as individualised tutoring.
- Choices, not a predetermined path, will decide the future.

## Excerpts

> "We started working on this report in 2022, before ChatGPT was released. At the time, I knew that we were in the early stages of a technology revolution that was vastly increasing the power of AI - I've been using LLMs for a while already."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, Stanford Digital Economy Lab Q&A, 22 November 2024 (dash in the original replaced with a hyphen)

> "We assembled some of the absolute top experts from academia and industry to put together a report on the implications for the workforce in the economy."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, Q&A

> "Now LLMs like ChatGPT have the potential to change that. I already know a lot of kids, and for that matter, adults, including a Nobel Laureate, who use these tools daily for individualized tutoring."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, Q&A

> "Our most important conclusion was that there is no predetermined future, but rather our choices will determine our future. In fact as the tools become more powerful, our choices become ever more consequential."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, Q&A

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- The "no predetermined future" line reads as a core stance, repeated in his later interviews.
- Stressing that choices grow more consequential as tools improve suggests he links capability growth to rising stakes for governance.
