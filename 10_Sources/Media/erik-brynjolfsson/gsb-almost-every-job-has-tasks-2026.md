# "Almost Every Job Has Tasks That AI Can Change" (2026)

## Citation

Bonos, L. "'Almost Every Job Has Tasks That AI Can Change'" (interview with Erik Brynjolfsson). Stanford GSB Insights, 17 September 2026. https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/almost-every-job-has-tasks-ai-can-change.

## One-line summary

Brynjolfsson says AI changes tasks rather than whole jobs, that organisations matter more than models, and that institutions may lag transformative AI.

## Key claims

- Almost no job is fully automatable, but almost every job contains tasks AI can change.
- Successful deployments depended on the organisation, not the model.
- Institutions take years to build and transformative AI may not wait.
- AI fluency is necessary but not sufficient.

## Excerpts

> "Almost no job is fully automatable, while almost every job has tasks that AI can change."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, Stanford GSB Insights, 17 September 2026

> "The key to success was almost never the AI model. It was the organization."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, Stanford GSB Insights, 17 September 2026

> "Institutions take years to construct, and transformative AI may not wait for them."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, Stanford GSB Insights, 17 September 2026

> "Fluency is important but it's not enough. Being AI-fluent in 2026 is like being email-fluent in 2005."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, Stanford GSB Insights, 17 September 2026

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- The task-level lens recurs across his work and reads as his default unit of analysis.
- Placing the bottleneck in organisations and institutions suggests he treats AI progress as a social adoption problem more than a technical one.
