# Economics of AI, Social Networks, and Technology

## Citation

Brynjolfsson, Erik, and Lex Fridman. "Economics of AI, Social Networks, and Technology". Lex Fridman Podcast, episode 141, 22 November 2020. https://lexfridman.com/erik-brynjolfsson/.

## One-line summary

Brynjolfsson discusses the exponential nature of digital technology, the limits of machine capability, and his conviction that humans, not technology, shape their destiny.

## Key claims

- Digital technologies make the world increasingly exponential.
- Many tasks, from care work to scientific and artistic creativity, remain beyond machines.
- Technology does not determine outcomes; human choices do.
- Most improvements in living standards come from technological progress.

## Excerpts

> "Our world is becoming more and more exponential, mainly because of digital technologies."
> ~ Lex Fridman Podcast 141, 2020

> "There's just too many things that machines still can't do. When I look around the world and think of whether it's child care or health care, clean the environment, interacting with people, scientific work, artistic creativity, these are things that for now, machines aren't able to do nearly as well as humans."
> ~ Lex Fridman Podcast 141, 2020

> "Technology doesn't shape our destiny. We shape our destiny."
> ~ Lex Fridman Podcast 141, 2020

> "Most improvements in living standards come from tech progress."
> ~ Lex Fridman Podcast 141, 2020

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- "We shape our destiny" is the conversational form of his later "AI has no predetermined future" argument.
- Listing the limits of machines anchors his complementarity case in concrete, non-automatable tasks.
- Crediting Solow on technological progress shows the through-line from his MIT lineage to his AI work.
