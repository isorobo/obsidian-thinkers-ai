# Erik Brynjolfsson on the Second Machine Age

## Citation

Brynjolfsson, Erik, and Russ Roberts. "Erik Brynjolfsson on the Second Machine Age". EconTalk, Library of Economics and Liberty, 3 February 2014. https://www.econtalk.org/brynjolfsson-on-the-second-machine-age/.

## One-line summary

Brynjolfsson discusses The Second Machine Age with Russ Roberts, arguing that smart machines bring both opportunity and a widening gap between the skilled and the unskilled.

## Key claims

- Done right, the machine age is good news, but the current setup can harm many people.
- Humans retain advantages in fine motor control and tasks machines cannot yet perform.
- The era rewards talented entrepreneurs while punishing those without special skills.
- Immigration policy should retain talented graduates trained in the United States.

## Excerpts

> "It should be good news if we do it right. But certainly the way we have things set up now, it can also be bad news for a lot of people."
> ~ Second Machine Age, EconTalk 2014

> "There is really probably no better time to be a talented entrepreneur, but there is also no worse time to be a person with no special skills or aptitudes."
> ~ Second Machine Age, EconTalk 2014

> "We should probably staple a green card to every Ph.D. diploma and encourage them to stay."
> ~ Second Machine Age, EconTalk 2014

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- The "good news if we do it right" framing previews the policy-fork stance he later formalises in the IMF essay.
- His emphasis on skill-based divergence shows that inequality has been central to his reading of technology since at least 2014.
- The green-card remark reveals a concrete, policy-minded streak alongside the macroeconomic theory.
