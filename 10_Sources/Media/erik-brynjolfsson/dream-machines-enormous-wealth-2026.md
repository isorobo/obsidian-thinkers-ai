# AI Will Create Enormous Wealth - But Who Benefits? (2026)

## Citation

Madrigal, A. and Sloan, R. "AI Will Create Enormous Wealth - But Who Benefits? with Erik Brynjolfsson" (interview with Erik Brynjolfsson). Dream Machines, KQED, 17 August 2026. https://www.kqed.org/news/12095464/ai-will-create-enormous-wealth-but-who-benefits-with-erik-brynjolfsson.

## One-line summary

Brynjolfsson says general purpose technologies drive growth, that AI favours scale and so concentrates wealth, and that human-machine teams should be the benchmark.

## Key claims

- General purpose technologies drive almost all economic growth.
- Intelligence alone does not determine economic power.
- AI works better at scale, which favours concentration.
- Capital is more concentrated than labour.
- Human-machine pairs often beat either alone.

## Excerpts

> "General purpose technology is pretty much what drives all economic growth. There's all these inventions, but there's just a handful that really raise our living standards."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, 00:06:03

> "Intelligence is not the be all and end all. A lot of very smart people think and wish it was, but when you get into the real world, you know, just look around us. There's there's lots of PhDs in the economy and they don't like, rule the economy."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, 00:06:56

> "The AI itself tends to work better at large scale. That's why they're raising hundreds of billions, trillions of dollars. You make the model bigger and it works better. So they said, let's make it even bigger."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, 00:27:04

> "Capital is inherently much more concentrated than labor. You know, no matter how smart you are, you can't run a whole fortune 500 corporation. So you delegate stuff."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, 00:28:41

> "How can a human and machine together do it? In many cases, the human machine can do better than the machine by itself or the human by itself."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, 00:31:59

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- He ties scaling economics to wealth concentration, which suggests he reads capability trends through distribution.
- Preferring "the design business" to prediction (as stated elsewhere in the episode) reads as a deliberate stance against forecasting.
