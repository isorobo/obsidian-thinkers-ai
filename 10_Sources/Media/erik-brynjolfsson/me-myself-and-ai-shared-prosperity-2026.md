# Creating Shared Prosperity With AI (2026)

## Citation

Ransbotham, S. "Creating Shared Prosperity With AI: Stanford Digital Economy Lab's Erik Brynjolfsson" (interview with Erik Brynjolfsson). Me, Myself, and AI, MIT Sloan Management Review, 21 July 2026. https://sloanreview.mit.edu/audio/creating-shared-prosperity-with-ai-stanford-digital-economy-labs-erik-brynjolfsson/.

## One-line summary

Brynjolfsson argues the future with AI is a matter of choice, that intangible investment dwarfs direct technology spend, and that AI amplifies whatever intent people bring.

## Key claims

- The right question is what people choose to do with AI, not what AI will do to them.
- Intangible complements are about ten times larger than direct computer investment.
- Measured productivity can fall at first as inputs rise without matching output.
- AI amplifies intention.

## Excerpts

> "Wait a minute, the premise of your question is wrong. It's 'What are we going to do with AI?'"
> ~ Brynjolfsson, framing the AI challenge

> "The investments in those intangible assets are about 10 times bigger than the direct investments in computer technology."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, on complementary assets

> "More input, no increase in output, [and] productivity, at least as it is conventionally measured, goes down."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, on the J-curve

> "AI is the greatest amplifier of intention ever. Whatever they want to achieve, AI can amplify it."
> ~ Brynjolfsson, on mindful optimism

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- He rejects technological determinism, which reads as a consistent stance across his work.
- Quantifying intangibles suggests he locates AI's payoff in organisational change, not hardware.
