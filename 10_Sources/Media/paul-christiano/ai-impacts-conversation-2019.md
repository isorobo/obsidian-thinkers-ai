# Conversation with Paul Christiano (2019)

## Citation

Bergal, A., Fernandez, R. and Long, R. "Conversation with Paul Christiano" (interview with Paul Christiano). AI Impacts, 13 August 2019. https://aiimpacts.org/conversation-with-paul-christiano/.

## One-line summary

In a transcribed conversation with AI Impacts researchers, Christiano gives his estimate of alignment-related doom and explains why he disagrees with MIRI about the core difficulty of the problem.

## Key claims

- He puts AI alignment per se at roughly 10% doominess.
- He doubts that society would work on a clean algorithmic problem for a decade and find it still hopeless.
- He rejects the claim that his approach merely pushes the core difficulty around.
- He sees adversarial training as close to working, with the gaps being a research problem.

## Excerpts

> "I don't know, it's a lot of doom probability. Like maybe I think AI alignment per se is like 10% doominess."
> ~ AI Impacts conversation, 13 August 2019

> "There just aren't many very clean algorithmic problems where our society worked on it for 10 years and then we're like, 'Oh geez, this still seems really hard.'"
> ~ AI Impacts conversation, 13 August 2019

> "Suppose I wanted to breed an animal modestly smarter than humans that is really docile and friendly. I'm like, I don't know man, that seems like it might work."
> ~ AI Impacts conversation, 13 August 2019, on the evolution analogy

> "Adversarial training, where I'm like, it seems close to working. And you could imagine it being like, it's just a research problem to fill in the gaps."
> ~ AI Impacts conversation, 13 August 2019

> "I don't grant the conception of the core difficulty in which this has just pushed around the core difficulty."
> ~ AI Impacts conversation, 13 August 2019, on disagreement with MIRI

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Reads as an empirical, analogy-testing style: he answers MIRI's evolution argument with a concrete breeding thought experiment.
- Suggests moderate optimism grounded in the view that alignment is an ordinary research problem.
