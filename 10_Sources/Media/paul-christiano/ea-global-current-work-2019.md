# Current work in AI alignment

## Citation

Christiano, Paul. "Current work in AI alignment". EA Global, San Francisco 2019. Transcript published 3 April 2020. https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/63stBTw3WAW6k45dY/paul-christiano-current-work-in-ai-alignment.

## One-line summary

Christiano maps the alignment research landscape: intent alignment as the target, the alignment tax as the cost, and the goal of turning any algorithm into an aligned version of itself.

## Key claims

- The target is intent alignment: building systems that are trying to do what you want them to do.
- The alignment tax is the cost incurred by insisting on alignment over raw capability.
- The research goal is to design align(X): a method that is intent-aligned, nearly as useful as X, and as scalable as X.
- Learning poses the challenge of making an aligned version of a potentially unaligned algorithm.
- Outer alignment means finding an objective that incentivises aligned behaviour.

## Excerpts

> "What I mean is 'intent alignment,' which is trying to build AI systems that are trying to do what you want them to do."
> ~ On the target of alignment

> "The alignment tax is intended to capture that gap — that cost that I incur if I insist on alignment."
> ~ On the alignment tax

> "I'm then trying to design a new algorithm, 'align(X)', which is intent-aligned, nearly as useful as X, and as easily scalable as X."
> ~ On the core research goal

> "Roughly speaking, what I mean by 'outer alignment' is finding an objective that incentivizes aligned behavior."
> ~ On outer alignment

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Christiano organises a sprawling field into a compact agenda built on one target and one cost.
- The align(X) framing shows his engineering instinct: transform existing methods rather than start from scratch.
- Naming the alignment tax reflects his attention to competitive pressure as the binding constraint.
