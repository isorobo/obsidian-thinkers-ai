# Paul Christiano on how OpenAI is developing real solutions to the AI alignment problem

## Citation

Christiano, Paul, and Robert Wiblin. "Paul Christiano on how OpenAI is developing real solutions to the 'AI alignment problem'". 80,000 Hours Podcast, episode 44, 2 October 2018. https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/paul-christiano-ai-alignment-solutions/.

## One-line summary

Christiano defines alignment as building systems that try to do what we want, traces the problem to competitive pressure, and explains iterated amplification as his proposed solution.

## Key claims

- Alignment is the problem of building AI systems that are trying to do what we want them to do.
- Competitive pressure to develop AI is the only reason the problem exists.
- An intelligence as smart as a crow could outcompete humans badly at some tasks.
- In iterated amplification a human uses several copies of the current AI as assistants as training proceeds.
- Real solutions, not just warnings, can be built at present-day labs.

## Excerpts

> "AI alignment, I see as the problem of building AI systems that are trying to do the thing that we want them to do."
> ~ On defining alignment

> "I think the competitive pressure to develop AI, in some sense, is the only reason there's a problem."
> ~ On the source of the problem

> "It is quite conceivable that an intelligence that was as smart as a crow...would actually outcompete humans pretty badly at those tasks."
> ~ On capability comparisons

> "Over the course of training, you have this AI that's getting more and more competent, the human at every point in time uses several copies of the current AI as assistants."
> ~ On iterated amplification

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Christiano frames alignment as engineering with real near-term solutions, not only a warning about risk.
- Tracing the problem to competitive pressure reflects his interest in the strategic, not only technical, structure of the challenge.
- His plain definition of alignment recurs unchanged across years of his public statements.
