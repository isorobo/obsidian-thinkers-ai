# Senate Testimony: Oversight of A.I. — Rules for Artificial Intelligence

## Citation

Marcus, Gary. "Senate Testimony". Oversight of A.I.: Rules for Artificial Intelligence, U.S. Senate Judiciary Subcommittee on Privacy, Technology, and the Law, 16 May 2023. https://www.judiciary.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/2023-05-16%20-%20Testimony%20-%20Marcus.pdf.

## One-line summary

Testifying alongside Sam Altman, Marcus warns that unreliable AI threatens democracy and calls for independent pre-deployment review and a global safety body.

## Key claims

- Systems that fluently confabulate enable election interference and market manipulation.
- Many risks stem from the inherent unreliability of current systems.
- Current systems do not honour the values of transparency, privacy, fairness, and safety.
- The big tech companies' plan reduces to "trust us", which is not a sufficient basis.
- AI needs independent scientific review before release, and ultimately a CERN-like global safety body.

## Excerpts

> "We should all be deeply worried about systems that can fluently confabulate, unburdened by reality."
> ~ Page 1

> "The big tech companies' preferred plan boils down to 'trust us'. Why should we?"
> ~ Page 2

> "Allowing independent scientists access to these systems before they are widely released – as part of a clinical trial-like safety evaluation - is a vital first step."
> ~ Page 3

> "Ultimately, we may need something like CERN, global, international, and neutral, but focused on AI safety, rather than high-energy physics."
> ~ Page 3

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Opening with a GPT-4-generated fake news story shows him demonstrating risk rather than asserting it.
- The clinical-trial and FDA-style framing makes reliability and pre-deployment review the heart of his policy view.
- Pairing distrust of corporate self-regulation with a call for independent scientists matches his outsider-critic stance.
