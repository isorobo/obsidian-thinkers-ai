# Gary Marcus Wants to Tame Silicon Valley

## Citation

Marcus, Gary, and Justin Hendrix. "Gary Marcus Wants to Tame Silicon Valley". Tech Policy Press, 22 September 2024. https://www.techpolicy.press/gary-marcus-wants-to-tame-silicon-valley/.

## One-line summary

In a book interview, Marcus argues that AI firms borrow intellectual property without paying, that systems break under novel reasoning, and that generative AI carries a high moral cost.

## Key claims

- AI companies do not pay for the intellectual property they borrow.
- Current systems fall apart when a task departs from training and requires reasoning.
- Using generative AI now carries a high moral cost.
- The harms of generative AI are not problems the companies know how to solve.
- Policy must shift the burden back onto the firms creating these systems.

## Excerpts

> "The companies aren't paying for any of it. They're not really paying people whose intellectual property they're borrowing."
> ~ On data and intellectual property

> "If you change from what you've been trained on, you really need to reason over it, these systems fall apart."
> ~ On reasoning limits

> "There is a moral cost to using generative AI right now. I think it's actually pretty high."
> ~ On the ethics of use

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- The intellectual-property point ties his capability critique to a concrete economic injustice.
- Repeating that systems fall apart off-distribution restates his core generalisation argument.
- Naming a high moral cost shows him extending technical critique into ethical judgement.
