# AI Debate: Gary Marcus and Yoshua Bengio on the Best Way Forward for AI

## Citation

Marcus, Gary, and Yoshua Bengio. "AI Debate". Montreal.AI Debate, Mila, Montreal, 23 December 2019. https://medium.com/@Montreal.AI/transcript-of-the-ai-debate-1e098eeb8465.

## One-line summary

Debating Bengio, Marcus argues that deep learning is one component among many and that symbols and causal reasoning remain essential.

## Key claims

- Deep learning is one element in a larger set of machinery, not a complete solution.
- Useful systems already mix rule-based representation with learned components.
- The meaning of a whole sentence cannot be crammed into a single vector.
- Deep learning lacks ways to represent causal relationships and to perform logical inference.
- Progress requires combining learning with symbolic and causal machinery.

## Excerpts

> "Deep Learning is just one element in a very complicated set of machinery."
> ~ On hybrid models

> "You can't cram the meaning of an entire sentence into a single vector."
> ~ On compositionality

> "Deep learning lacks ways of causal relationships and has no obvious way of performing logical inference."
> ~ On deep learning limits

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Casting deep learning as one element among many is the framing the 2020 paper later builds out.
- The single-vector objection restates his long-running argument about compositional structure.
- Pairing causation with logical inference shows the two capacities he treats as missing from pure learning.
