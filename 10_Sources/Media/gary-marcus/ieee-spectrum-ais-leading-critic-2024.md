# How and Why Gary Marcus Became AI's Leading Critic

## Citation

Marcus, Gary, and Eliza Strickland. "How and Why Gary Marcus Became AI's Leading Critic". IEEE Spectrum, 17 September 2024. https://spectrum.ieee.org/gary-marcus.

## One-line summary

Interviewed by IEEE Spectrum, Marcus argues that scaling will not fix LLM unreliability and points to the U.S. Senate as the place that could prove his cynicism wrong.

## Key claims

- LLMs smear words together and produce statements that are true only some of the time.
- The current approach keeps hitting the same problems repeatedly.
- There is good reason to think scaling alone will not work.
- Meaningful regulation would have to address the full list of concerns he has raised.
- He invites the Senate to disprove his pessimism by acting on those concerns.

## Excerpts

> "These systems smear together a lot of words, and they come up with things that are true sometimes and not others."
> ~ On LLM limits

> "We have very good reason to think that merely scaling things up will not work."
> ~ On scaling as a solution

> "The place that I'd like to prove me wrong is the U.S. Senate...If the Senate passed something that actually addressed all 12, then my cynicism would have been mislaid."
> ~ On regulation and policy

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Describing LLMs as smearing words restates his comprehension-versus-fluency distinction in plain terms.
- The repeated-problems point reflects his view that limits are structural, not incidental bugs.
- Naming the Senate as his test for being wrong ties his capability critique back to his policy programme.
