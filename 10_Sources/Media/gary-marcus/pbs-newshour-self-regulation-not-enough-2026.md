# Self-regulation 'not enough' for AI safety, Gary Marcus says (2026)

## Citation

Bennett, G. "Self-regulation 'not enough' for AI safety, Gary Marcus says" (interview with Gary Marcus). PBS NewsHour, 29 September 2026. https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/self-regulation-not-enough-for-ai-safety-gary-marcus-says.

## One-line summary

Marcus tells PBS that extinction is unlikely but catastrophe may be likely, that company self-regulation is insufficient, and that the China race is overblown.

## Key claims

- Extinction scenarios assume humans will not fight back.
- Catastrophe is more likely than extinction.
- Self-regulation is not enough.
- China has licensing rules, so the race argument is overblown.

## Excerpts

> "So I don't think extinction is a real likely risk for humanity. All of the scenarios, the so-called doom scenarios where AI just wipes us out like in 'The Terminator,' all assume that we won't fight back, and we will."
> ~ PBS NewsHour interview, extinction risk answer

> "So extinction is not likely, but catastrophe might be likely."
> ~ PBS NewsHour interview, realistic concerns answer

> "It's certainly not enough. And trusting these companies is insane."
> ~ PBS NewsHour interview, self-regulation answer

> "I think the race against China is a little bit overblown. China actually does have regulation, has licensing regime around its own software products."
> ~ PBS NewsHour interview, China answer

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Reads as a consistent split between extinction (rejected) and catastrophe (accepted), which shapes his regulatory case.
- Suggests he answers the competitiveness argument with a factual claim about foreign regulation.
