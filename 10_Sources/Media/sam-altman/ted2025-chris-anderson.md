# OpenAI's Sam Altman Talks ChatGPT, AI Agents and Superintelligence (TED2025)

## Citation

Anderson, C. (host), Altman, S. (guest). "OpenAI's Sam Altman Talks ChatGPT, AI Agents and Superintelligence". TED2025, 11 April 2025. https://www.ted.com/talks/sam_altman_openai_s_sam_altman_talks_chatgpt_ai_agents_and_superintelligence_live_at_ted2025.

## One-line summary

In conversation with Chris Anderson, Altman defines safety as a product requirement and commits OpenAI to releasing a frontier open-source model.

## Key claims

- Altman states OpenAI wants to build tools that lift up human creativity rather than replace it.
- OpenAI commits to releasing a powerful open-source model near the capability frontier.
- ChatGPT's growth outpaces any other product Altman has been involved with.
- Scientists report measurable productivity gains from using AI in research.
- Safety is framed as a product requirement rather than a separate constraint.
- Altman expects his children to grow up in a world of highly capable AI products by default.

## Excerpts

> "The creative spirit of humanity is an incredibly important thing. And we want to build tools that lift that up"
> ~ On AI's creative impact

> "We're going to do a very powerful open source model...something near the frontier, I think better than any current open source model out there."
> ~ On open-source commitment

> "I have never seen growth in any company, one that I've been involved with or not like this, like the growth of ChatGPT."
> ~ On ChatGPT's growth

> "A good product is a safe product. You will not use our agents if you do not trust that they're not going to like empty your bank account or delete your data."
> ~ On safety as a product feature

> "My kids...will never grow up in a world where products and services are not incredibly smart, incredibly capable."
> ~ On future capabilities

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Collapses the traditional safety-versus-product tension by defining safety as a commercial necessity.
- Signals a shift toward open-weight releases, a departure from OpenAI's earlier closed-model posture.
- Uses personal, familial framing to make abstract AGI claims concrete for a public audience.
