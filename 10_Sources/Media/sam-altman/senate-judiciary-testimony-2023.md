# Senate Judiciary Testimony - Sam Altman (2023)

## Citation

Altman, S. (2023). *Written Testimony of Sam Altman, Chief Executive Officer, OpenAI*. Hearing before the US Senate Judiciary Subcommittee on Privacy, Technology, and the Law, 16 May 2023.

## One-line summary

Altman's first congressional testimony calls for a licensing regime for the most powerful AI models and endorses government oversight of frontier development.

## Key claims

- Regulatory intervention by governments is critical to mitigate risks from increasingly powerful AI models.
- A US or global agency should license the most capable AI systems.
- The licensing agency should hold the power to revoke a licence for non-compliance.
- OpenAI already restricts hateful, harassing, violent, and adult content through its use policies.
- Altman states his worst fear is that the AI field causes significant harm to the world.
- Poorly governed AI development carries a risk of going badly wrong at scale.

## Excerpts

> "We think that regulatory intervention by governments will be critical to mitigate the risks of increasingly powerful models."
> ~ Opening statement

> "My worst fears are that we cause, we the field, the technology, the industry, cause significant harm to the world."
> ~ Response to committee questioning

> "If this technology goes wrong, it can go quite wrong."
> ~ Response to committee questioning

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Altman opens his public regulatory posture by inviting oversight, a position he narrows by the 2025 Senate Commerce hearing.
- Frames AI risk in serious terms while simultaneously promoting deployment, a duality he maintains across later talks.
- Proposes agency licensing consistently in this and later interviews, showing a stable early policy preference.
