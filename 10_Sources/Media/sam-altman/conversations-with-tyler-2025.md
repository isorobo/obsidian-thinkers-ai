# Sam Altman on Trust, Persuasion, and the Future of Intelligence

## Citation

Cowen, T. (host), Altman, S. (guest). "Sam Altman on Trust, Persuasion, and the Future of Intelligence". Conversations with Tyler, live at the Progress Conference, 17 October 2025. https://conversationswithtyler.com/episodes/sam-altman-2/.

## One-line summary

Altman names subtle, unintentional AI persuasion as his clearest safety concern and reframes the compute bottleneck as an energy bottleneck.

## Key claims

- Most people misjudge how well they allocate their own time.
- OpenAI's hardware team increasingly resembles a research organisation rather than a chip company.
- Altman envisions people delegating routine coordination to personal AI agents.
- He expects a future model, referred to as GPT-6, to conduct genuine new science.
- He names subtle, unintentional AI persuasion as a serious safety concern.
- Energy generation, not chip supply alone, is described as the binding constraint on AI growth.

## Excerpts

> "People almost never allocate their time as well as they think they do."
> ~ On productivity and delegation

> "Our chip team feels more like the OpenAI research team than a chip company."
> ~ On hardware strategy

> "GPT-6 can really do it"
> ~ On GPT-6's scientific potential, contrasted with GPT-5's "tiny glimmers"

> "The AI models accidentally take over the world...just subtly convinces you of something. No intention, it just does."
> ~ On accidental AI persuasion

> "We need to make more electrons"
> ~ On energy as a computing constraint

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Names unintentional persuasion, rather than deliberate takeover, as his clearest AGI safety concern.
- Reframes the compute bottleneck as an energy bottleneck, extending his infrastructure advocacy.
- Treats internal hardware work as continuous with core AI research, not a separate business line.
