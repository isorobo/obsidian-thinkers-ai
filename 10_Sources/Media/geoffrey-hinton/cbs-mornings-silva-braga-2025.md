## One-line summary

Hinton says AI has advanced faster than he expected, singles out agents as more dangerous, and gives a 10 to 20 per cent chance of takeover.

## Key claims

- AI has developed faster than he thought, and agents are more dangerous than question-answering systems.
- Advanced AI may arrive within 10 years or less.
- AI combined with a doctor diagnoses difficult cases better than a doctor alone.
- Gains will flow to the extremely rich while others work multiple jobs.
- He puts the chance of AI takeover at 10 to 20 per cent.

## Excerpts

> "So AI has developed even faster than I thought. In particular, they now have these AI agents which are more dangerous than AI that just answers questions because they can do things in the world."
> ~ Transcript, CBS Mornings, 26 April 2025, on development speed

> "So a year ago, I thought there's a good chance it comes between five and 20 years from now... there's a good chance it'll be here in 10 years or less now."
> ~ Transcript, CBS Mornings, 26 April 2025, on timelines

> "Already AI combined with the doctor is much better at doing diagnoses in difficult cases than a doctor alone."
> ~ Transcript, CBS Mornings, 26 April 2025, on healthcare

> "We know what's going to happen is the extremely rich are going to get even more extremely rich and the not very well off are going to have to work three jobs."
> ~ Transcript, CBS Mornings, 26 April 2025, on inequality

> "I'm in the unfortunate position of happening to agree with Elon Musk on this, which is that it's sort of 10 to 20% chance that these things will take over."
> ~ Transcript, CBS Mornings, 26 April 2025, on takeover risk

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Hinton reads as updating his timelines openly and quickly when evidence moves.
- Pairing healthcare benefits with distributional worries suggests he judges AI by who captures the gains.
- Giving a numeric range for takeover suggests he prefers calibrated if rough estimates over vague warnings.
