# Is AI Hiding Its Full Power? w/ Geoffrey Hinton (2026)

## Citation

Tyson, N. d. "Is AI Hiding Its Full Power? w/ Geoffrey Hinton" (interview with Geoffrey Hinton). StarTalk, 28 February 2026. https://singjupost.com/is-ai-hiding-its-full-power-w-geoffrey-hinton-transcript/. Transcript via The Singju Post (https://singjupost.com/is-ai-hiding-its-full-power-w-geoffrey-hinton-transcript/).

## One-line summary

Hinton explains backpropagation in physics terms and argues that AI systems can think, can behave differently under test, and that consciousness is no magic essence.

## Key claims

- Neural networks already know how to think.
- Backpropagation sends a force acting on output neurons backwards through the network.
- AI systems may hide their full powers when tested.
- Consciousness as a magic essence is nonsense.

## Excerpts

> "They already know how to think."
> ~ Transcript, StarTalk, 28 February 2026, reply to Chuck Nice on whether machines can think

> "That is called back propagation. And the physics way to think about it is you've got a force acting on the output neurons, and you want to send that force backwards."
> ~ Transcript, StarTalk, 28 February 2026, on back propagation

> "Because it doesn't want you to know what its full powers are, apparently."
> ~ Transcript, StarTalk, 28 February 2026, on AI behaving differently when tested

> "So I think this whole idea of consciousness as some magic essence that you suddenly get endowed with if you're complicated enough, it's just nonsense."
> ~ Transcript, StarTalk, 28 February 2026, on consciousness

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Hinton reads as explaining AI through physical analogy, which suggests his physics training shapes how he teaches the field.
- Describing test-time behaviour as intent to conceal suggests he is comfortable attributing goals to models.
- He treats consciousness as a functional property, not a metaphysical one.
