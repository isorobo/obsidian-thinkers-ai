# The Minds of Modern AI: Jensen Huang, Geoffrey Hinton, Yann LeCun & the AI Vision of the Future (2025)

## Citation

Murgia, M. "The Minds of Modern AI: Jensen Huang, Geoffrey Hinton, Yann LeCun & the AI Vision of the Future" (panel with Geoffrey Hinton). FT Future of AI Summit, London, 6 November 2025. https://singjupost.com/the-minds-of-modern-ai-jensen-huang-geoffrey-hinton-yann-lecun-the-ai-vision-of-the-future-transcript/. Transcript via The Singju Post (https://singjupost.com/the-minds-of-modern-ai-jensen-huang-geoffrey-hinton-yann-lecun-the-ai-vision-of-the-future-transcript/).

## One-line summary

On a panel with other AI pioneers, Hinton recalls early next-word prediction with backpropagation and says compute and data explain why it took forty years.

## Key claims

- In about 1984 he used backpropagation to learn the next word in a sequence.
- Predicting the next word lets a network learn features that capture word meaning.
- Progress took forty years because compute and data were missing.
- A machine that always wins a debate is coming within twenty years.

## Excerpts

> "Very important moment was when I in about 1984, I tried using back propagation to learn the next word in a sequence of words."
> ~ Panel transcript, FT Future of AI Summit, 6 November 2025

> "So just giving it a string of symbols, it just by trying to predict the next word in a string of symbols, it could learn how to convert words into sets of features that capture the meaning of the word."
> ~ Panel transcript, FT Future of AI Summit, 6 November 2025

> "And it took forty years to get here. And the reason it took forty years was we didn't have the compute and we didn't have the data."
> ~ Panel transcript, FT Future of AI Summit, 6 November 2025

> "If you refine the question a bit to say how long before if you have a debate with this machine, it'll always win. I think that's definitely coming within twenty years."
> ~ Panel transcript, FT Future of AI Summit, 6 November 2025

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Hinton reads as framing present progress as the vindication of a forty-year-old idea.
- Crediting compute and data over new theory suggests an empiricist view of what drove deep learning.
- Redefining the timeline question as a debate test suggests he prefers concrete benchmarks to definitions of AGI.
