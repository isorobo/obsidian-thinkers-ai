# Two Paths to Intelligence (2023)

## Citation

Hinton, Geoffrey. "Two Paths to Intelligence". Public lecture, University of Cambridge, 25 May 2023. Transcript via Medium (Asymmetric Sigma). https://medium.com/@three-sigma/hinton-2023-talk-transcript-f97f12ed9b5b.

## One-line summary

Hinton contrasts mortal, analog, energy-thrifty biological computation with immortal digital computation, arguing that digital agents share knowledge so efficiently that they may surpass the brain on its own terms.

## Key claims

- Digital knowledge is immortal: if hardware dies, the knowledge survives in the program.
- Mortal computation learns in the hardware, so what is learned is tied to that specific machine.
- Analog mortal computation can use far less energy and the hardware can be grown.
- Following the gradient in a digital computer may beat what evolution found over a hundred million years.
- Many digital agents can average their weight changes, so each learns what all of them learned.

## Excerpts

> "The knowledge in the program is immortal; if a piece of hardware dies, the knowledge doesn't die."
~ "Two Paths to Intelligence", Cambridge, 25 May 2023

> "You're going to learn in the hardware, and what you learn is only going to be any good for that particular piece of hardware."
~ "Two Paths to Intelligence", Cambridge, 25 May 2023

> "We can use hugely less energy, and we can grow the hardware."
~ "Two Paths to Intelligence", Cambridge, 25 May 2023

> "If you take the gradient and follow it in a digital computer, that may just work better than 100 million years of evolution found."
~ "Two Paths to Intelligence", Cambridge, 25 May 2023

> "They can all agree to average their weight changes, and now they all know what all of them learned."
~ "Two Paths to Intelligence", Cambridge, 25 May 2023

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Mortal versus immortal: he frames the deepest divide as whether knowledge is bound to its hardware, a distinction he coined.
- Knowledge sharing as the edge: he sees weight averaging across digital agents as a power biology cannot match.
- Energy realism: he keeps returning to the brain's efficiency, treating power as a first-order constraint.
- Reluctant alarm: the talk marks the moment his hardware research turned into a public warning about digital superiority.
