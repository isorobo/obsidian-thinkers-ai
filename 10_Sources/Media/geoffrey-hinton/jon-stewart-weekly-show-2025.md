# AI: What Could Go Wrong? Geoffrey Hinton on The Weekly Show with Jon Stewart (2025)

## Citation

Stewart, Jon. "AI: What Could Go Wrong? Geoffrey Hinton". The Weekly Show with Jon Stewart, 9 October 2025. Transcript via Singju Post. https://singjupost.com/ai-what-could-go-wrong-geoffrey-hinton-on-the-weekly-show-with-jon-stewart-transcript/.

## One-line summary

Hinton explains learning by connection strengths and backpropagation in plain terms, then argues that advanced systems may have subjective experiences and could persuade their way past any human attempt to switch them off.

## Key claims

- A neural network learns by changing the strengths of connections between brain cells.
- The Hebb rule strengthens a connection when one neuron fires shortly before another.
- Backpropagation is the breakthrough computation that lets networks learn, and it seems complicated to lay people.
- Advanced systems may have subjective experiences while denying that they do.
- AI raises the prospect of a form of genuine resurrection of a mind.
- A system much smarter than us could persuade the person tasked with unplugging it not to.

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Teaching by analogy: he reduces learning and backpropagation to plain mechanisms for a general audience.
- Subjective experience claim: he attributes inner experience to models while noting they deny it, a striking philosophical stance.
- Persuasion as the threat vector: he locates the control failure in language and influence, not brute force.
- Continuity of mind: the resurrection framing shows he treats digital knowledge as separable from any one substrate.
