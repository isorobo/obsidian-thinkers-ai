# The Godfather of AI says we can't afford to get it wrong (2025)

## Citation

Chakrabarti, Meghna. "The 'Godfather of AI' says we can't afford to get it wrong". On Point, WBUR, 29 December 2025. https://www.wbur.org/onpoint/2025/12/29/godfather-of-ai-geoffrey-hinton.

## One-line summary

Hinton recalls the early dismissal of neural networks, describes chatbot memory as reconstruction rather than stored text, and argues the development of AI cannot be stopped because the incentives are too strong.

## Key claims

- Most people in AI and computer science once thought his approach was nonsense.
- Knowledge in a chatbot does not sit in stored strings of words.
- Remembering is making things up and getting them right.
- These systems sit somewhere we have no prior experience of.
- The development of AI could not be stopped because the profits and benefits are too large.

## Excerpts

> "Most people doing artificial intelligence and most people doing computer science thought it was nonsense."
~ On Point, WBUR, 29 December 2025

> "The knowledge doesn't sit around in words, and so when you get a chatbot, for example, and when it learns, it doesn't remember any strings of words."
~ On Point, WBUR, 29 December 2025

> "Remembering is when you just make stuff up and get it right."
~ On Point, WBUR, 29 December 2025

> "It's somewhere in between. We're dealing with something where we have no experience of this kind of thing before."
~ On Point, WBUR, 29 December 2025

> "I don't believe it could be stopped. There's too many profits and too many good things would come out of it for us to stop the development of AI."
~ On Point, WBUR, 29 December 2025

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Vindication narrative: he recalls early dismissal, framing his career as a long bet that paid off.
- Distributed knowledge: he insists knowledge lives in weights, not stored words, against a filing-cabinet view of memory.
- Reconstruction theme: he repeats that remembering is plausible fabrication, a recurring claim across his late interviews.
- Inevitability: he doubts any pause, citing profits and benefits, consistent with his stance elsewhere.
