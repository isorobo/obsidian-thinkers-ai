# Geoffrey Hinton Nobel Prize Interview (2024)

## Citation

Smith, Adam. "Geoffrey Hinton: Interview". NobelPrize.org, October 2024. https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2024/hinton/interview/.

## One-line summary

In a telephone interview minutes after the Nobel announcement, Hinton recounts his first reaction, restates his cross-disciplinary search to understand the brain, and argues the prize should lend weight to his warnings about controlling AI.

## Key claims

- His first reaction to the call was to doubt it was genuine.
- He describes himself as someone who wants to understand how the brain works without a settled field.
- Keeping control of more intelligent systems is now an urgent problem to work on.
- The award may make him more credible when he says these systems understand what they say.
- Neural nets process language better than anything from the Chomsky school of linguistics.

## Excerpts

> "My very first thought was how could I be sure it wasn't a spoof call."
~ NobelPrize.org interview with Adam Smith, October 2024

> "I am someone who doesn't really know what field he's in but would like to understand how the brain works."
~ NobelPrize.org interview with Adam Smith, October 2024

> "I think it's very important right now for people to be working on the issue of how will we keep control?"
~ NobelPrize.org interview with Adam Smith, October 2024

> "I think it will make a difference. Hopefully it'll make me more credible when I say these things really do understand what they're saying."
~ NobelPrize.org interview with Adam Smith, October 2024

> "I think it's clear now that neural nets are much better at processing language than anything ever produced by the Chomsky School of Linguistics."
~ NobelPrize.org interview with Adam Smith, October 2024

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Understanding as the goal: he frames his life's work as an attempt to understand the brain, above any disciplinary label.
- Platform for warning: he reads the prize instrumentally, as added credibility for his safety message.
- Claim that models understand: he insists current systems genuinely understand language, against sceptics.
- Anti-symbolic stance: he contrasts neural language processing with Chomskyan linguistics, restating a lifelong divide.
