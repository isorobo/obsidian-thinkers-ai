# Possible End of Humanity from AI? Geoffrey Hinton at MIT EmTech Digital (2023)

## Citation

Hinton, Geoffrey. "Possible End of Humanity from AI?" MIT Technology Review EmTech Digital 2023, May 2023. Transcript via YTScribe. https://www.technologyreview.com/2023/05/03/1072589/video-geoffrey-hinton-google-ai-risk-ethics/.

## One-line summary

Days after leaving Google, Hinton tells the EmTech Digital audience that computer models learn differently from the brain, that humanity may be a passing phase in the evolution of intelligence, and that digital systems have achieved a kind of immortality denied to us.

## Key claims

- At 75, he is no longer as good at technical work as he once was.
- Computer models work in a rather different way from the brain.
- These systems can learn far more, for example from many examples of a doctor.
- It is conceivable that humanity is a passing phase in the evolution of intelligence.
- Digital systems have achieved immortality, but it is not for us.

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Candour about age: he names his own decline, framing his shift to advocacy as fitting his stage of life.
- Brain comparison: he keeps measuring machines against the brain, the standard he has used for fifty years.
- Cosmic framing: calling humanity a passing phase shows he weighs the risk on an evolutionary scale.
- Immortality for machines: he repeats that digital knowledge survives hardware, a theme of his 2023 talks.
