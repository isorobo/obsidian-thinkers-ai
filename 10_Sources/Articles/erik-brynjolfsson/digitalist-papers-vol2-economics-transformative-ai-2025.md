# Introduction: The Economics of Transformative AI (2025)

## Citation

Susskind, D., Brynjolfsson, E., Korinek, A., Pentland, A. and Agrawal, A. "Introduction: The Economics of Transformative AI". The Digitalist Papers, Volume 2, Stanford Digital Economy Lab, December 2025. https://www.digitalistpapers.com/intro/etai.

## One-line summary

The introduction to Volume 2 defines transformative AI as a change comparable to the industrial revolution on a much shorter timescale and frames the volume around it.

## Key claims

- Recent AI progress and record private investment motivate the volume.
- Transformative AI is defined by economic transformation comparable to the industrial revolution but faster.
- The volume addresses transformative AI rather than the current state of AI.

## Excerpts

> "ChatGPT became the fastest-growing consumer app of all time. Soon after, a wave of similar AI systems - Claude, Gemini, and Grok - followed, each built in a similar spirit with rapidly improving capabilities."
> ~ Section I, The Context for the Volume (dash in the original replaced with a hyphen)

> "In 2024, global private investment in AI hit a record high, reaching $150.79 billion."
> ~ Section I, Investment Scale

> "We define 'TAI' as advanced AI systems that will usher in an economic transformation comparable to the industrial revolution, but on a much shorter timescale."
> ~ Section II, Purpose Definition

> "This is not, then, a volume about the current state of AI and its implications, but TAI and what it would mean."
> ~ Section II, Volume Focus

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Defining TAI by economic outcome rather than by capability threshold suggests an economist's operational framing of advanced AI.
- The industrial revolution comparison with a compressed timescale reads as a speed claim without a stated takeoff mechanism.
