# Will Generative Artificial Intelligence Deliver on Its Promise in Health Care?

## Citation

Wachter, Robert M., and Erik Brynjolfsson. "Will Generative Artificial Intelligence Deliver on Its Promise in Health Care?". JAMA 331, no. 1 (2024): 65-69. Published online 30 November 2023. https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2023.25054.

## One-line summary

Wachter and Brynjolfsson argue that generative AI's speed and self-correction will shorten the usual lag between technological promise and results in health care.

## Key claims

- General purpose technologies often fail to deliver promised benefits for years, the productivity paradox of information technology.
- Health care has attributes that make new technology deployment harder than in other industries.
- Generative AI's rapid improvement and complementary innovation make faster gains likely than in prior technology waves.
- The scale of low-value health spending makes enthusiasm for the technology natural.

## Excerpts

> "History has shown that general purpose technologies often fail to deliver their promised benefits for many years ('the productivity paradox of information technology')."
> ~ Abstract

> "The ability of genAI to rapidly improve and the capacity of organizations to implement complementary innovations that allow IT tools to reach their potential are more advanced than in the past."
> ~ Abstract

> "In a field in which an estimated 30% of the $4.3 trillion spent each year in the US adds little to no value... it is natural to be enthusiastic about genAI's potential."
> ~ Body

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Brynjolfsson applies his J-curve and complementary-investment framework to a specific sector, health care.
- The argument that genAI may break the historical lag refines, rather than abandons, his productivity-paradox thesis.
- Co-authoring with a clinical leader shows his method of pairing economic theory with domain expertise.
