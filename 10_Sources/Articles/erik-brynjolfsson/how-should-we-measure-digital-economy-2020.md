# How Should We Measure the Digital Economy?

## Citation

Brynjolfsson, Erik, and Avinash Collis. "How Should We Measure the Digital Economy?". Hutchins Center Working Paper #57, Brookings Institution, January 2020. https://www.brookings.edu/research/how-should-we-measure-the-digital-economy/.

## One-line summary

Brynjolfsson and Collis argue that free internet services escape GDP, and they propose measuring consumer surplus through choice experiments to capture the welfare they create.

## Key claims

- Free internet services go largely uncounted in GDP and productivity statistics.
- The information sector has stayed at 4-5% of GDP for 40 years despite the digital explosion.
- GDP measures production, not well-being; consumer surplus is the better welfare measure.
- Choice experiments can estimate consumer surplus at scale, including for free goods.
- The median United States consumer values Wikipedia at around $150 per year while paying nothing.

## Excerpts

> "Because so many of the internet services we each use are free, they largely go uncounted in official measures of economic activity such as GDP and Productivity."

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- The accessible framing shows his intent to move measurement debates from journals into policy.
- The "everywhere except in the GDP statistics" line echoes Solow, fixing his work in that lineage.
- Proposing to supplement rather than replace GDP reveals a pragmatic, additive reform instinct.
