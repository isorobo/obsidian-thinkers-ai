# The Macroeconomics of Artificial Intelligence

## Citation

Brynjolfsson, Erik, and Gabriel Unger. "The Macroeconomics of Artificial Intelligence". IMF Finance & Development 60, no. 4 (December 2023): 20-25. https://www.imf.org/en/publications/fandd/issues/2023/12/macroeconomics-of-artificial-intelligence-brynjolfsson-unger.

## One-line summary

Brynjolfsson and Unger argue that AI has no predetermined economic future and that today's policy choices will steer it between a high-productivity, lower-inequality path and a low-productivity, concentrated one.

## Key claims

- AI's economic effects fork into divergent futures across productivity growth, the labour market, and industrial concentration.
- The bad future is the path of least resistance; the good future requires deliberate policy.
- AI risks becoming a narrow labour-saving "so-so technology" if adoption stays slow and confined to large firms.
- Solow's productivity paradox may intensify if firms fail to make the needed organisational changes.
- Society should set positive goals for AI, not only outcomes to avoid.

## Excerpts

> "The collective decisions we make today will determine how AI affects productivity growth, income inequality, and industrial concentration."
> ~ Standfirst

> "AI does not have a predetermined future. It can develop in very different directions."
> ~ Section "First fork: Productivity growth"

> "The path that leads to a worse future is the one of least resistance and results in low productivity growth, higher income inequality, and higher industrial concentration."
> ~ Pull quote

> "More than any other factor, productivity-output per unit of input-determines the wealth of nations and the living standards of their people."
> ~ Section "First fork: Productivity growth"

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- The "fork in the road" device restates his core conviction that technological outcomes are chosen, not fated.
- Coupling productivity, inequality, and concentration in one frame shows he treats AI as a distributional and structural question, not only a growth question.
- His self-aware caution about economists' forecasting record reflects a measurement-first temperament even when making a normative argument.
