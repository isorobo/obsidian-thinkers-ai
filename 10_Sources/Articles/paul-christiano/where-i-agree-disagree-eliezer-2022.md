# Where I agree and disagree with Eliezer

## Citation

Christiano, Paul. "Where I agree and disagree with Eliezer". AI Alignment Forum, 19 June 2022. https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/CoZhXrhpQxpy9xw9y/where-i-agree-and-disagree-with-eliezer.

## One-line summary

Christiano responds to Yudkowsky's list of lethalities, agreeing that catastrophic AI could arrive soon while disagreeing that alignment cannot be learned through experiment and iteration.

## Key claims

- Catastrophically risky AI could plausibly exist soon, with no strong consensus until the risk is large.
- Humanity usually solves technical problems by iterating and fixing failures.
- Solving alignment without learning from experiments would be incredibly hard, but such learning is available.
- Yudkowsky equivocates between alignment being hard and alignment being impossible in time.
- Yudkowsky's record on scientific prediction includes the wrong call on the Higgs boson.

## Excerpts

> "Catastrophically risky AI systems could plausibly exist soon, and there likely won't be a strong consensus about this fact until such systems pose a meaningful existential risk per year."
> ~ Agreement point

> "Humanity usually solves technical problems by iterating and fixing failures; we often resolve tough methodological disagreements very slowly by seeing what actually works."
> ~ Agreement point

> "Solving a scientific problem without being able to learn from experiments and failures is incredibly hard. But we will be able to learn a lot about alignment from experiments and trial and error."
> ~ Disagreement point

> "The Eliezer predictions most relevant to 'how do scientific disciplines work' that I'm most aware of are incorrectly predicting that physicists would be wrong about the existence of the Higgs boson."
> ~ Disagreement point

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Christiano grounds optimism in empiricism: alignment is a science problem open to trial and error.
- He distinguishes "hard" from "impossible in time", refusing to collapse difficulty into doom.
- The point-by-point format shows his preference for precise disagreement over global verdicts.
