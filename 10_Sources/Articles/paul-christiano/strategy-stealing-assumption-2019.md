# The strategy-stealing assumption (2019)

## Citation

Christiano, P. "The strategy-stealing assumption". AI Alignment Forum, 16 September 2019. https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/nRAMpjnb6Z4Qv3imF/the-strategy-stealing-assumption.

## One-line summary

Christiano proposes that humans can mimic any strategy an unaligned AI uses to gain influence, treats this as a working approximation, and lists failure modes where it breaks.

## Key claims

- For any strategy an unaligned AI uses to influence the long-run future, a similarly sized group of humans has an analogous strategy.
- He thinks the assumption is not strictly true but is a good enough approximation to reason from.
- Companies do antisocial things because influential humans want them to.
- The alignment goal is to ensure AI is compatible with strategy-stealing.

## Excerpts

> "for any strategy an unaligned AI could use to influence the long-run future, there is an analogous strategy that a similarly-sized group of humans can use in order to capture a similar amount of flexible influence over the future."
> ~ The strategy-stealing assumption, 16 September 2019, opening statement

> "I think strategy-stealing isn't really true; but I think it's a good enough approximation that we can basically act as if it's true, and then think about the risk posed by possible failures."
> ~ The strategy-stealing assumption, 16 September 2019

> "Companies do things that seem antisocial not because they have come unmoored from any human's values, but because plenty of influential humans want them to do that."
> ~ The strategy-stealing assumption, 16 September 2019, on institutional failures

> "we just want to ensure that AI is compatible with strategy-stealing"
> ~ The strategy-stealing assumption, 16 September 2019, conclusion

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Reads as a habit of stating a clean idealised assumption, then auditing where it fails.
- Suggests he lowers the alignment goalpost to a more tractable requirement rather than demanding full value alignment.
