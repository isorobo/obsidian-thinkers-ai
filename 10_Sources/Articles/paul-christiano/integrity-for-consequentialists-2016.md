# Integrity for consequentialists

## Citation

Christiano, Paul. "Integrity for consequentialists". The sideways view, 14 November 2016. https://sideways-view.com/2016/11/14/integrity-for-consequentialists/.

## One-line summary

Christiano argues that a consequentialist should value integrity by acting as if each choice reveals the kind of person they are, because credible commitments enable positive-sum trade.

## Key claims

- Choosing an action should be imagined as revealing to everyone the kind of person who picks it.
- Integrity exceeds keeping one's word: it means being someone others are glad to have dealt with.
- The capacity to commit is a precondition for most positive-sum trade.
- Integrity should be straightforward, not justified by clever reasoning about exceptions.
- A consequentialist gains from integrity precisely through the trust it secures.

## Excerpts

> "I make one change: when I imagine picking an action, I pretend that picking it causes everyone to know that I am the kind of person who picks that option."
> ~ On the decision rule

> "Integrity doesn't mean merely keeping my word. To the extent I want to interact with you I will be the kind of person you will be predictably glad to have interacted with."
> ~ On what integrity means

> "Being able to make commitments is useful, as a precondition for most kinds of positive-sum trade."
> ~ On why commitment matters

> "I'm not going to use some complicated reasoning to explain why screwing you over is consistent with integrity, I am just going to be straightforward."
> ~ On avoiding motivated reasoning

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Christiano applies decision-theoretic reasoning to ethics, deriving honesty from incentives rather than rules.
- The reflexive framing, treating a choice as a signal of one's type, mirrors the commitment logic in his alignment proposals.
- The essay shows the same trust-and-cooperation concerns that later shape his thinking about AI and humans.
