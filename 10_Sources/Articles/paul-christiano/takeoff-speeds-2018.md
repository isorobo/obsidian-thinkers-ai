# Takeoff speeds

## Citation

Christiano, Paul. "Takeoff speeds". The sideways view, 24 February 2018. https://sideways-view.com/2018/02/24/takeoff-speeds/.

## One-line summary

Christiano argues for slow, continuous takeoff: world output will double over a four-year interval before it doubles over any one-year interval, because slightly worse AI precedes the best AI.

## Key claims

- Slow takeoff means a complete four-year doubling of world output precedes the first one-year doubling.
- Before an incredibly intelligent AI, we will probably have a slightly worse one.
- Builders optimise for usefulness, so progress in usefulness is more likely to be continuous.
- A twelve-month lead in AGI is as hard to convert to advantage as a thirty-year lead in aircraft.
- Researchers will optimise aggressively for general intelligence when it helps the tasks they care about.

## Excerpts

> "There will be a complete 4 year interval in which world output doubles, before the first 1 year interval in which world output doubles."
> ~ Definition of slow takeoff

> "Before we have an incredibly intelligent AI, we will probably have a slightly worse AI."
> ~ The basic argument for continuity

> "When humans build AI, they will be optimizing for usefulness, and so progress in usefulness is much more likely to be linear."
> ~ On why progress is continuous

> "Being 12 months ahead in AGI might get you a decisive strategic advantage, but the world has accelerated so much that that's just about as hard as getting to airplanes 30 years before anyone else."
> ~ On strategic implications

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Christiano operationalises a fuzzy debate with a crisp, falsifiable doubling criterion.
- The continuity argument flows from economics, not speculation about AI agency.
- The essay frames his long-running disagreement with Yudkowsky over discontinuous takeoff.
