# Clarifying "AI Alignment"

## Citation

Christiano, Paul. "Clarifying "AI Alignment"". AI Alignment Forum, 15 November 2018 (originally 7 April 2018). https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/ZeE7EKHTFMBs8eMxn/clarifying-ai-alignment.

## One-line summary

Christiano defines alignment narrowly as the AI trying to do what its operator wants, separating motivation from knowledge and ability.

## Key claims

- An AI is aligned with an operator when it is trying to do what the operator wants it to do.
- The alignment problem is building powerful systems aligned with their operators.
- Alignment concerns the motives of the assistant, not its knowledge or competence.
- An aligned AI tries to work out what is right and, like a human, may or may not succeed.
- This intent-alignment framing scopes the problem away from solving ethics or capability.

## Excerpts

> "A is trying to do what H wants it to do."
> ~ Core definition of alignment

> "The alignment problem is the problem of building powerful AI systems that are aligned with their operators."
> ~ Statement of the problem

> "I use alignment as a statement about the motives of the assistant, not about their knowledge or ability."
> ~ Scoping the definition

> "An aligned AI would try to figure out which thing is right, and like a human it may or may not succeed."
> ~ On knowledge versus motivation

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Christiano insists on a precise, narrow definition before building, a habit that shapes the whole prosaic-alignment programme.
- Separating motivation from competence lets him treat alignment as tractable without first solving ethics.
- The "trying to do what we want" formula recurs verbatim across his talks and interviews.
