# Should We Worry More About Short-Term AI Risk or Long-Term AI Risk?

## Citation

Marcus, Gary. "Should We Worry More About Short-Term AI Risk or Long-Term AI Risk?". Marcus on AI (Substack), 14 March 2023. https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/should-we-worry-more-about-short.

## One-line summary

Marcus holds that short-term harms like misinformation and long-term risks both demand action now, and refuses to rank one decisively above the other.

## Key claims

- Misinformation could overwhelm democracy soon unless countered now.
- He can no longer decide whether short-term or long-term risks worry him more.
- Short-term risks include misinformation and bias; long-term risks include powerful systems with messy values.
- Humanity cannot act as if nothing is happening or as if it has infinite time.
- Either society accelerates its adaptation or it makes an enforceable decision to slow development.

## Excerpts

> "If we don't start fighting now, democracy may well be overwhelmed by misinformation and consequent polarization—and perhaps quite soon."
> ~ Quoting his Atlantic essay

> "Being honest, I can no longer even decide whether I am more concerned about the short-term risks ... or the long-term risks."
> ~ Body

> "We can't just act as if nothing is happening here. And we can't act like we have infinite time to decide what to do, either."
> ~ Conclusion

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Refusing to rank the risks shows him resisting the short-versus-long-term framing common in the field.
- Putting misinformation and democracy first reflects his focus on near-term societal harm.
- The call to act now without infinite time matches the urgency of his policy testimony.
