# Muddles about Models (2023)

## Citation

Marcus, G. "Muddles about Models". Marcus on AI (Substack), 5 October 2023. https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/muddles-about-models.

## One-line summary

Marcus disputes a claim that language models hold literal world models, arguing that correlation with space or time is not a model.

## Key claims

- Correlation with space or time does not show a genuine representation of either.
- The point of a model is downstream use in tasks such as robotics or navigation.
- A system can hold correlations in word space with no valid model of a person's life.
- Safety will not emerge from systems that substitute correlation for structured representation.

## Excerpts

> "Finding that some stuff correlates with space or time doesn't mean that stuff genuinely represents space or time."
> ~ Section on the Gurnee and Tegmark paper

> "Correlating where cities are with what's said about them is no substitute for a map."
> ~ Caption beneath the Atlantic Ocean image

> "The whole point of models is to be able to use them downstream for other purposes, e.g, in robotics, navigation, computer graphics."
> ~ Ernie Davis commentary section

> "Safety is not going to emerge anytime soon from hit-or-miss systems that substitute correlation for a factual, structured, interrogable representation."
> ~ Final paragraphs

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Reads as a test of representation by downstream use, not by probing results.
- Suggests he treats interpretability evidence with caution when it rests on correlation.
