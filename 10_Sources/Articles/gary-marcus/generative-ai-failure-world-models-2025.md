# Generative AI's crippling and widespread failure to induce robust models of the world (2025)

## Citation

Marcus, G. "Generative AI's crippling and widespread failure to induce robust models of the world". Marcus on AI (Substack), 28 June 2025. https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/generative-ais-crippling-and-widespread.

## One-line summary

Marcus argues that language models fail to build stable world models, using chess as a window, and that relying on them is a safety risk.

## Key claims

- A world model is a persistent, stable and updatable internal representation of entities.
- Language models lose track in chess and make illegal moves.
- He says their output comes from mimicry, not abstract cognition over world models.
- He says trusting such systems with our lives is risky.

## Excerpts

> "World models are persistent, stable, updatable (and ideally up-to-date) internal representations of some set of entities within some slice of the world."
> ~ Definition of world models

> "By the midgame, LLMs often get lost. Not only does the quality of play diminish deeper into the game, but LLMs start to make illegal moves."
> ~ Chess section

> "Everything that they do is through mimicry, rather than abstracted cognition across proper world models."
> ~ Section on the fundamental problem

> "Leaving our lives in the hands of LLMs that lack explicit models is literally putting our lives at risk."
> ~ Safety implications

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Reads as an argument that one concrete, checkable failure (illegal chess moves) generalises to a structural flaw.
- Suggests he links representational limits directly to safety claims.
