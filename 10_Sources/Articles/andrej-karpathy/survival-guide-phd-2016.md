# A Survival Guide to a PhD

## Citation

Karpathy, Andrej. "A Survival Guide to a PhD". karpathy.github.io, 2016. https://karpathy.github.io/2016/09/07/phd/.

## One-line summary

Karpathy's guide to ML PhD research argues that problem selection is more important than problem solving, that a 10x more important problem is at most 2-3x harder to achieve, and that authentic contribution matters more than gaming academic metrics.

## Key claims

- PhD research operates on a meta level: most time goes to identifying which problems are worth solving, not to solving them.
- A 10x more important problem is at most 2-3x harder to achieve, so ambitious problem selection has asymmetric returns.
- Following Richard Hamming's framework, a problem must have a reasonable attack to be worth pursuing, regardless of its importance in the abstract.
- Good research problems are fertile: they allow subsequent work to chain from the same foundation.
- Incremental work that achieves 2% benchmark gains does not generate impact, citations, or lasting contribution.
- Academia is small and interconnected; gaming metrics through self-citation or manipulation fails in the long run.
- Research is a social endeavour: conferences matter for networking, not for talks.

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Karpathy's insistence on ambitious problem selection with a feasible attack foreshadows his preference for big conceptual moves (LLM OS, Software 2.0, vibe coding) over incremental technical papers.
- His warning against benchmark-gaming reflects the same empirical honesty visible in his ImageNet self-experiment and his later essay on evals and verifiability.
- The emphasis on taste, implicit community knowledge, and tacit understanding of "what is important" anticipates his views on education and the limits of formal instruction, which underpin Eureka Labs.
