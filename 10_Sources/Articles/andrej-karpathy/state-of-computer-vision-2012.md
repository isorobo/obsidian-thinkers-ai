# The State of Computer Vision and AI: We Are Really, Really Far Away

## Citation

Karpathy, Andrej. "The State of Computer Vision and AI: We Are Really, Really Far Away". karpathy.github.io, 2012. https://karpathy.github.io/2012/10/22/state-of-computer-vision/.

## One-line summary

Karpathy argues in 2012 that AI and computer vision lag catastrophically behind human scene understanding, and that data and algorithms alone will not close the gap without embodied experience and the right training signal.

## Key claims

- Current benchmarks such as ImageNet and Pascal VOC test only narrow labelling tasks, not genuine scene understanding.
- Machines lack the inferential abilities humans use automatically: 3D reasoning, object affordances, identity recognition, theory of mind, and temporal prediction.
- More data and better optimisation algorithms are insufficient; the field needs the right form of training signal, not merely more of the same.
- Embodiment and extended temporal experience may be necessary preconditions for human-level scene understanding.
- The gap between machine capability and the task ahead is so large that the author describes it as depressing.

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Karpathy held a deeply pessimistic view of AI capability in 2012, which contrasts with his later optimism about LLMs; the evolution tracks the arrival of deep learning and scaling.
- He identified the embodiment hypothesis early, anticipating debates about grounding and world models that run through his later LLM OS framing.
- His critique of narrow benchmarks anticipates his later emphasis on evals and real-world capability, not toy metrics.
- The post is a baseline document: reading it alongside his 2025 essays reveals the full arc of his changing views on AI capability.
