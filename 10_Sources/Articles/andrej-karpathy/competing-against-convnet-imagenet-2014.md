# What I Learned from Competing Against a ConvNet on ImageNet

## Citation

Karpathy, Andrej. "What I Learned from Competing Against a ConvNet on ImageNet". karpathy.github.io, 2014. https://karpathy.github.io/2014/09/02/what-i-learned-from-competing-against-a-convnet-on-imagenet/.

## One-line summary

Karpathy personally classified 1,500 ImageNet images to benchmark himself against GoogLeNet, finding humans still edge out ConvNets but only through sustained expert effort, and that the two systems fail in complementary ways.

## Key claims

- A trained human annotator achieves 5.1% top-5 error on ImageNet versus GoogLeNet's 6.8%, a statistically significant but narrow margin.
- Human advantage requires significant effort, expertise, and time; casual performance degrades quickly.
- Human accuracy is not a single point but a tradeoff curve that varies by expertise and effort invested.
- Humans outperform ConvNets on small or thin objects, image filters, and abstract representations; ConvNets outperform humans on fine-grained distinctions such as dog breeds with over 120 categories.
- The complementary failure modes suggest that progress requires understanding both human and machine error, not simply pushing a single benchmark number.

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Karpathy approaches capability questions empirically: he personally ran the experiment rather than speculating, a pattern visible throughout his career.
- His interest in human-machine comparison anticipates his 2025 framing of LLMs as digital minds with distinct strengths, not simple human replacements.
- The note that human advantage is eroding foreshadows his later view that AI will surpass human performance on knowledge work, which underpins his economic-impact positions.
