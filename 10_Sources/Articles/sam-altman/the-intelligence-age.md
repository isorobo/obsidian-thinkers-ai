# The Intelligence Age

## Citation

Altman, Sam. "The Intelligence Age". Personal blog, 23 September 2024. https://ia.samaltman.com/.

## One-line summary

Altman declares deep learning's predictable scaling has opened an "Intelligence Age" that could bring superintelligence within a few thousand days.

## Key claims

- Deep learning improved predictably with scale across the past decade.
- Superintelligence may arrive within a few thousand days.
- Greater compute and data availability directly expands what AI can help people solve.
- Insufficient infrastructure would turn AI into a scarce resource that nations fight over.
- Personal AI teams of virtual experts will help individuals tackle problems they could not solve alone.
- The transition will not be entirely positive, but the potential upside justifies navigating the risks.

## Excerpts

> "In the next couple of decades, we will be able to do things that would have seemed like magic to our grandparents."
> ~ Opening

> "Deep learning worked, got predictably better with scale, and we dedicated increasing resources to it."
> ~ On the path to this moment

> "It is possible that we will have superintelligence in a few thousand days."
> ~ On historical significance

> "If we don't build enough infrastructure, AI will be a very limited resource that wars get fought over."
> ~ On infrastructure

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Restates the "abundance through compute" thesis first proposed in Moore's Law for Everything, three years on.
- Treats predictable scaling as an empirical law, underwriting his short AGI timeline.
- Frames infrastructure buildout as a geopolitical necessity, foreshadowing his later compute and energy advocacy.
