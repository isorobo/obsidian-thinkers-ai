# What I Wish Someone Had Told Me

## Citation

Altman, Sam. "What I Wish Someone Had Told Me". Personal blog, 22 December 2023. https://blog.samaltman.com/what-i-wish-someone-had-told-me.

## One-line summary

Altman lists seventeen operating principles for founders, emphasising concentrated bets, fast execution, and compounding advantage.

## Key claims

- Optimism, obsession, self-belief, raw effort, and personal connections are what start successful ventures.
- Concentrating resources on a small number of high-conviction bets is simple to state and hard to practise.
- Long-range plans should span decades while execution moves in weeks.
- Inspiration fades quickly, so inaction is a hidden but serious risk.
- Businesses that compound an advantage with scale generate outsized long-term value.

## Excerpts

> "Optimism, obsession, self-belief, raw horsepower and personal connections are how things get started."
> ~ Point 1

> "Concentrate your resources on a small number of high-conviction bets; this is easy to say but evidently hard to do."
> ~ Point 5

> "Plans should be measured in decades, execution should be measured in weeks."
> ~ Point 11

> "Inspiration is perishable and life goes by fast. Inaction is a particularly insidious type of risk."
> ~ Point 13

> "Compounding exponentials are magic. In particular, you really want to build a business that gets a compounding advantage with scale."
> ~ Point 15

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Shows the operating philosophy carried over from Y Combinator into how Altman runs OpenAI at speed.
- Prioritises decisive, concentrated bets over diversified hedging, consistent with OpenAI's compute-heavy strategy.
- Treats compounding advantage as a central objective, echoing the scaling arguments made later in Three Observations.
