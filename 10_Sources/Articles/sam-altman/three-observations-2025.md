# Three Observations

## Citation

Altman, Sam. "Three Observations". Personal blog, February 2025. https://blog.samaltman.com/three-observations.

## One-line summary

Altman sets out three empirical observations on AI scaling and cost, and argues they justify continued exponential investment in compute.

## Key claims

- Model intelligence scales roughly with the log of the resources used to train and run it.
- The cost of a fixed level of AI capability falls about tenfold every twelve months.
- Falling prices drive dramatically more use, shown by the GPT-4-to-GPT-4o price drop.
- The socioeconomic value of steadily increasing intelligence grows super-exponentially.
- Continued exponential investment in AI rests on economic logic, not only on speculation.
- Rising AI capability could unsettle the balance between capital and labour, requiring new distribution mechanisms.

## Excerpts

> "The intelligence of an AI model roughly equals the log of the resources used to train and run it."
> ~ Observation 1

> "The cost to use a given level of AI falls about 10x every 12 months, and lower prices lead to much more use."
> ~ Observation 2

> "The socioeconomic value of linearly increasing intelligence is super-exponential in nature."
> ~ Observation 3

> "Anyone in 2035 should be able to marshall the intellectual capacity equivalent to everyone in 2025."
> ~ Closing vision

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Converts qualitative AGI optimism into a quantified economic model, maturing the argument first sketched in Moore's Law for Everything.
- Uses the log-scaling observation to justify sustained capital expenditure, supporting OpenAI's infrastructure strategy.
- Continues to flag capital-labour imbalance as a policy problem without committing to a specific redistribution mechanism.
