# Eliciting Latent Knowledge: How to tell if your eyes deceive you

## Citation

Christiano, Paul, Mark Xu, and Ajeya Cotra. "Eliciting Latent Knowledge: How to tell if your eyes deceive you". Alignment Research Center technical report, 14 December 2021. https://www.alignment.org/blog/arcs-first-technical-report-eliciting-latent-knowledge/.

## One-line summary

The report names Eliciting Latent Knowledge as a central alignment problem: training an AI to honestly report what it believes when it knows things the supervisor does not.

## Key claims

- The core difficulty is mapping between an AI's model of the world and a human's model.
- ELK is an open problem the authors believe is central to alignment.
- The report's main contribution is many candidate approaches plus a precise account of why the problem is hard and important.
- The research method pits a "builder" proposing a strategy against a "breaker" constructing counterexamples.
- A reporter that answers from the human's simulated model, rather than the AI's, would defeat naive training schemes.

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- ELK marks Christiano's shift from scalable oversight to a deeper worry: a system that knows the truth yet reports what humans expect to hear.
- The builder-breaker method formalises the adversarial habit running through his debate and amplification work.
- Founding the problem at ARC signals his move from algorithm proposals toward isolating the hardest unsolved core of alignment.
