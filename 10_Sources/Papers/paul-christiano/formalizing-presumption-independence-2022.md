# Formalizing the presumption of independence (2022)

## Citation

Christiano, P., Neyman, E. and Xu, M. "Formalizing the presumption of independence". arXiv, 12 November 2022. https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.06738.

## One-line summary

The paper asks whether the informal habit of defaulting to E[XY] = E[X]E[Y] can be turned into a formal heuristic estimator that accepts valid arguments and rejects spurious ones.

## Key claims

- Deduction can be used to make uncertain estimates that stay open to revision.
- A default estimate of E[XY] = E[X]E[Y] is a key ingredient of such reasoning, called the presumption of independence.
- This style of reasoning is common and often successful, but informal.
- The main open problem is whether a heuristic estimator can formalise valid uses of the presumption without accepting spurious arguments.

## Excerpts

> "Mathematical proof aims to deliver confident conclusions, but a very similar process of deduction can be used to make uncertain estimates that are open to revision."
> ~ Abstract, opening sentence

> "in the absence of any specific information about the correlation between X and Y, which we call *the presumption of independence*."
> ~ Abstract, second sentence

> "Reasoning based on this heuristic is commonplace, intuitively compelling, and often quite successful -- but completely informal."
> ~ Abstract, third sentence

> "we present our main open problem: is there a heuristic estimator that formalizes intuitively valid applications of the presumption of independence without also accepting spurious arguments?"
> ~ Abstract, final sentence

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Reads as a preference for turning informal reasoning habits into precise, checkable mathematical objects.
- Suggests that the alignment agenda is approached through foundational theory, with an explicitly stated open problem.
