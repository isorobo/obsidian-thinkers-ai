# Deep reinforcement learning from human preferences

## Citation

Christiano, Paul, Jan Leike, Tom B. Brown, Miljan Martic, Shane Legg, and Dario Amodei. "Deep reinforcement learning from human preferences". Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2017. https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03741.

## One-line summary

The paper trains reinforcement learning agents from human comparisons between trajectory segments, solving complex tasks without a hand-coded reward function while querying humans on under one percent of interactions.

## Key claims

- Goals can be specified through non-expert human preferences between pairs of trajectory segments rather than an explicit reward function.
- The method solves Atari games and simulated robot locomotion while collecting feedback on less than one percent of agent interactions.
- The feedback cost is low enough to apply human oversight to state-of-the-art reinforcement learning systems.
- Novel behaviours can be trained with roughly one hour of human time.
- The behaviours and environments learned exceed in complexity anything previously learned from human feedback.

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Christiano frames alignment as a learning problem: the reward signal itself is learned from human judgement rather than specified by hand, the seed of his later scalable-oversight agenda.
- The emphasis on feedback efficiency shows his concern that human oversight must scale to systems far faster than their supervisors.
- Co-authorship with Leike and Amodei places this work at the start of the OpenAI alignment programme that produced iterated amplification and debate.
