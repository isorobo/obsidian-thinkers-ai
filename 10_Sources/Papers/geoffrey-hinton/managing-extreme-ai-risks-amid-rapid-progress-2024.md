# Managing extreme AI risks amid rapid progress (2024)

## Citation

Bengio, Y., Hinton, G., Yao, A., Song, D., Abbeel, P., Darrell, T., Harari, Y. N., Zhang, Y.-Q., Xue, L., Shalev-Shwartz, S. et al. "Managing extreme AI risks amid rapid progress". Science, 2024 (arXiv submitted 26 October 2023, last revised 22 May 2024). https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17688.

## One-line summary

A consensus paper by a large group of AI researchers that warns of large-scale harms and loss of human control, and calls for technical safety research combined with adaptive governance.

## Key claims

- AI is progressing rapidly and companies are shifting to generalist systems.
- The risks include large-scale social harms, malicious uses and irreversible loss of human control.
- AI safety research is lagging and present governance lacks the needed mechanisms and institutions.
- The authors propose a plan that combines technical research with proactive, adaptive governance.

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Hinton's co-signature suggests he is willing to pair technical authority with explicit governance demands.
- The stress on lagging safety research reads as a judgement that capability work outpaces risk work.
- The admission of no consensus on how risks arise suggests a preference for hedged, plan-based action over certainty claims.
