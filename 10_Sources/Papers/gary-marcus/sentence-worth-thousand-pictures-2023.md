# A Sentence is Worth a Thousand Pictures (2023)

## Citation

Leivada, E., Marcus, G., Günther, F. and Murphy, E. "A Sentence is Worth a Thousand Pictures: Can Large Language Models Understand Hum4n L4ngu4ge and the W0rld behind W0rds?". arXiv, submitted 26 July 2023, revised 4 September 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00109.

## One-line summary

The authors argue that language models lack grounded cognition and test this with a "leet" decoding task, where humans do well and models struggle.

## Key claims

- Next-word prediction shows great potential for language tasks, but the claims about it need assessment.
- Models lack grounded cognition, so they rely on fixed associations between words and word vectors.
- A novel leet task, with letters replaced by numbers, was used to test this.
- Humans excel in the task and models struggle.

## Excerpts

> "Modern Artificial Intelligence applications show great potential for language-related tasks that rely on next-word prediction."
> ~ Abstract, opening sentence

> "To assess these claims, first we analyze the contribution of LLMs as theoretically informative representations of a target cognitive system vs. atheoretical mechanistic tools."
> ~ Abstract

> "We hypothesize that since models lack grounded cognition, they cannot take advantage of these features and instead solely rely on fixed associations between represented words and word vectors."
> ~ Abstract

> "We designed and ran a novel 'leet task' (l33t t4sk), which requires decoding sentences in which letters are systematically replaced by numbers."
> ~ Abstract

> "The results suggest that humans excel in this task whereas models struggle, confirming our hypothesis."
> ~ Abstract, closing sentence

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Reads as a preference for testing a cognitive hypothesis with a small, designed experiment over leaning on benchmark scores.
- Suggests he frames the debate as one about what models are theoretically, not only what they do.
