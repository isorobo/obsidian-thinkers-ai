# Deep Learning: A Critical Appraisal

## Citation

Marcus, Gary. "Deep Learning: A Critical Appraisal". arXiv preprint arXiv:1801.00631, 2 January 2018. https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.00631.

## One-line summary

Marcus catalogues ten limitations of deep learning and argues the approach must be supplemented by other techniques to reach artificial general intelligence.

## Key claims

- Deep learning is a statistical pattern-classification technique, powerful at interpolation but weak at extrapolation beyond the training distribution.
- Deep learning is data hungry: humans learn abstract relationships from a handful of examples, whereas networks need vast labelled sets.
- Generalisation splits into interpolation between known examples and extrapolation beyond them; networks struggle with the latter.
- The field may be approaching diminishing returns, with leading figures beginning to voice the same concern.
- Deep learning cannot serve as a general solution to artificial intelligence and must be combined with other methods.

## Excerpts

> "I present ten concerns for deep learning, and suggest that deep learning must be supplemented by other techniques if we are to reach artificial general intelligence."
> ~ Abstract

> "Deep learning, as it is primarily used, is essentially a statistical technique for classifying patterns, based on sample data, using neural networks with multiple layers."
> ~ Section 2

> "Deep learning's limitations begin with the contrapositive: we live in a world in which data are never infinite."
> ~ Section 3

> "Human beings can learn abstract relationships in a few trials."
> ~ Section 3.1, Deep learning thus far is data hungry

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Marcus frames the critique through cognitive and developmental psychology: human few-shot learning becomes the benchmark deep learning fails to meet.
- The interpolation-versus-extrapolation distinction shows his core thesis that statistical learning cannot deliver systematic generalisation.
- Calling for deep learning to be "supplemented by other techniques" foreshadows the neurosymbolic hybrid argument he develops in later work.
