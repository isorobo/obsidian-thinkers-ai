# Innateness, AlphaZero, and Artificial Intelligence

## Citation

Marcus, Gary. "Innateness, AlphaZero, and Artificial Intelligence". arXiv preprint arXiv:1801.05667, 17 January 2018. https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.05667.

## One-line summary

Marcus argues that the "tabula rasa" framing of AlphaGo Zero is overstated and that AI, like biological minds, needs significant innate machinery.

## Key claims

- The Silver et al. claim that AlphaGo and successors reach superhuman level "starting tabula rasa" is overstated for multiple reasons.
- Biology supplies overwhelming evidence that humans and other creatures are born with significant innate machinery.
- Cognition can be modelled as a function of innate algorithms, innate representational formats, innate knowledge, and experience.
- A great deal of innate endowment is learning machinery: innateness enables learning rather than competing with it.
- AI needs greater attention to innateness, including abstract representational priors.

## Excerpts

> "I argue that these claims are overstated, for multiple reasons. I close by arguing that artificial intelligence needs greater attention to innateness."
> ~ Abstract

> "Virtually all modern observers would concede that genes and experience work together; it is 'nature and nurture', not 'nature versus nurture'."
> ~ Section 1, An ancient debate, updated for the machine age

> "There is ample reason to believe that humans and many other creatures are born with significant amounts of innate machinery."
> ~ Section 1

> "One might think of cognition as a function over four variables ... where a = innate algorithms ... r = innate representational formats ... k = innate knowledge ... and e = experience."
> ~ Section 3, Innateness in machines

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- The cognition = f(a, r, k, e) formalism shows Marcus recasting the AI debate as a quantitative question about how much should be innate.
- Documenting the October 2017 LeCun debate places him on the nativist side of a long-running empiricist-versus-nativist divide.
- Drawing on Plato, Chomsky, Locke, and Spelke ties his AI critique to the broader history of the nature-nurture question.
