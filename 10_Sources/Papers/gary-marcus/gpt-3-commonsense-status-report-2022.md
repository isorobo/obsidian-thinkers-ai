# Experiments in Commonsense Reasoning in GPT-3: Status Report from June 2022

## Citation

Davis, Ernest, and Gary Marcus. "Experiments in Commonsense Reasoning in GPT-3: Status Report from June 2022". Technical report, New York University, 7 June 2022. https://cs.nyu.edu/~davise/papers/GPT-3-6-22.html.

## One-line summary

Re-running the 2020 commonsense probes, Davis and Marcus find GPT-3 less bizarre but only slightly better on strict grading, undercutting the claim of steady scaling gains.

## Key claims

- GPT-3 improved between 2020 and 2022: the later version less often gives bizarre answers or wanders onto tangents.
- On strict grading the improvement on this example set is only slight.
- The 2022 version is less variable than the 2020 version, even at high randomisation settings.
- Machine learning tends to improve on average on its target metric, but does not improve monotonically.
- Two years of scaling did not close the commonsense reasoning gap.

## Excerpts

> "There has certainly been some improvement in GPT-3 over the last two years. The 2022 version is much less apt than 2020 to give risibly bizarre answers or to wander off onto strange tangents."
> ~ Status report body

> "However, if the responses are graded on a strict scale...there has been only a slight improvement on this set of examples between 2020 and 2022."
> ~ Status report body

> "Machine learning based technology does tend to improve on average on whatever metric its creators consider important...but it is by no means the case that it improves monotonically."
> ~ Status report body

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- The two-year re-test shows Marcus tracking capability claims longitudinally rather than reacting to single demos.
- Conceding genuine improvement while denying the gap has closed signals a critic who weighs evidence both ways.
- The monotonicity point sharpens his standing argument that scaling yields uneven, not guaranteed, progress.
