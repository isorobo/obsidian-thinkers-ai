# The Next Decade in AI: Four Steps Towards Robust Artificial Intelligence

## Citation

Marcus, Gary. "The Next Decade in AI: Four Steps Towards Robust Artificial Intelligence". arXiv preprint arXiv:2002.06177, 17 February 2020. https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06177.

## One-line summary

Marcus proposes a hybrid, knowledge-driven, reasoning-based approach centred on cognitive models as the route to robust AI, against the prevailing bet on scale and compute.

## Key claims

- Robust AI is intelligence that applies what it knows to a wide range of problems in a systematic and reliable way, transferring knowledge across contexts.
- Reliability is a prerequisite for trust: an AI that cannot be counted on to behave reliably should not be trusted.
- Deep learning techniques to date are data hungry, shallow, brittle, and limited in their ability to generalise.
- The field needs a hybrid architecture, large-scale abstract and causal knowledge, reasoning, and cognitive models.
- "Pointillistic" intelligence that works in many cases but fails unpredictably in similar ones falls short of robustness.

## Excerpts

> "I propose a hybrid, knowledge-driven, reasoning-based approach, centered around cognitive models, that could provide the substrate for a richer, more robust AI than is currently possible."

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- The paper makes reliability and transfer, not raw benchmark scores, the measure of progress, consistent with Marcus's reliability-before-alignment stance.
- The four pillars - hybrid architecture, abstract and causal knowledge, reasoning, and cognitive models - lay out his neurosymbolic alternative in full.
- Opening with Kant and William James signals that he grounds machine intelligence in a long nativist and cognitive tradition rather than in scaling trends.
