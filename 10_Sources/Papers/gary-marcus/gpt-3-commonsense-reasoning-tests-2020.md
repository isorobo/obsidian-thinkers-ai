# GPT-3: Commonsense Reasoning (157 Tests)

## Citation

Marcus, Gary, and Ernest Davis. "GPT-3: Commonsense Reasoning". Technical supplement, New York University, August 2020. https://cs.nyu.edu/~davise/papers/GPT3CompleteTests.html.

## One-line summary

Marcus and Davis document 157 hand-built probes of GPT-3, showing the model fails on biological, physical, spatial, and social commonsense despite fluent prose.

## Key claims

- GPT-3 fails on a large share of carefully built commonsense probes, despite producing fluent continuations.
- The tests were designed to be difficult for current natural language processing and pre-screened on AI Dungeon.
- Raw success counts are not a benchmark and should not be used to measure progress in automated commonsense reasoning.
- Failure spans biological consequences, physical constraints, spatial relationships, and social understanding.
- Fluent text generation does not entail underlying comprehension.

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- The empirical, example-by-example method shows Marcus testing grand capability claims against concrete failures rather than aggregate scores.
- Refusing to treat the counts as a benchmark reflects his insistence on rigour about what evaluation can and cannot show.
- The focus on commonsense reasoning extends his standing thesis that statistical language models lack genuine world models.
