# Generative AI at Work

## Citation

Brynjolfsson, Erik, Danielle Li, and Lindsey R. Raymond. "Generative AI at Work". National Bureau of Economic Research Working Paper No. 31161, April 2023, revised November 2023. https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161.

## One-line summary

A field study of 5,179 customer support agents finds that access to a generative AI assistant raises productivity by 14% on average, with the largest gains accruing to novice and low-skilled workers.

## Key claims

- Access to the generative AI tool increased issues resolved per hour by 14% on average.
- Gains concentrated among less-experienced workers, with a 34% improvement for novice and low-skilled agents and minimal impact on the most experienced.
- The AI raised productivity by capturing and disseminating the behaviour patterns of the most productive agents.
- Generative AI automates tasks that rely on tacit knowledge, which earlier waves of computerisation could not codify.
- AI assistance also improved customer sentiment, increased employee retention, and may produce worker learning.

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- The paper grounds Brynjolfsson's complementarity thesis in field evidence: AI augments lower-skilled workers rather than simply displacing them.
- Framing the result as the dissemination of tacit knowledge shows his long-standing interest in why prior computerisation failed to lift productivity, a thread from his 1990s productivity-paradox work.
- The focus on heterogeneity across workers, not an average effect alone, reflects his distributional reading of technological change and its bearing on inequality.
