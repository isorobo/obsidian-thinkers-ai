# Artificial Intelligence and the Modern Productivity Paradox: A Clash of Expectations and Statistics

## Citation

Brynjolfsson, Erik, Daniel Rock, and Chad Syverson. "Artificial Intelligence and the Modern Productivity Paradox: A Clash of Expectations and Statistics". National Bureau of Economic Research Working Paper No. 24001, November 2017. https://www.nber.org/papers/w24001.

## One-line summary

The authors reconcile AI's impressive capabilities with stagnant measured productivity, arguing that implementation lags, not false hopes, best explain the gap.

## Key claims

- AI matches or surpasses human performance in more domains, yet measured productivity growth has halved over a decade.
- Four explanations compete: false hopes, mismeasurement, redistribution, and implementation lags.
- Implementation lags are the largest contributor to the paradox.
- Full effects await waves of complementary innovation, modelled as intangible capital.
- Forward-looking optimism and backward-looking disappointment can coexist during technological restructuring.

## Excerpts

> "We live in an age of paradox. Systems using artificial intelligence match or surpass human level performance in more and more domains... Yet measured productivity growth has declined by half over the past decade."
> ~ Abstract

> "We thus appear to be facing a redux of the Solow (1987) Paradox: we see transformative new technologies everywhere but in the productivity statistics."
> ~ Section 1 (Introduction)

> "There is no inherent inconsistency between forward-looking technological optimism and backward-looking disappointment. Both can simultaneously exist."
> ~ Section 1 (Introduction)

> "Their full effects won't be realized until waves of complementary innovations are developed and implemented."
> ~ Abstract

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- The paper is the conceptual seedbed of the J-curve, with Larry Summers credited for suggesting the analogy.
- Brynjolfsson treats the paradox as a measurement and timing problem, not evidence against AI's promise.
- Coexisting optimism and disappointment foreshadow his later "fork in the road" framing of AI's future.
