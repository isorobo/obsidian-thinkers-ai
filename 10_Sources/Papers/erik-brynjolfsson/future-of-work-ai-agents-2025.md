# Future of Work with AI Agents: Auditing Automation and Augmentation Potential across the U.S. Workforce

## Citation

Shao, Yijia, Humishka Zope, Yucheng Jiang, Jiaxin Pei, David Nguyen, Erik Brynjolfsson, and Diyi Yang. "Future of Work with AI Agents: Auditing Automation and Augmentation Potential across the U.S. Workforce". arXiv preprint, 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06576.

## One-line summary

The paper introduces an auditing framework and the WORKBank database to measure which occupational tasks workers want AI agents to automate or augment, set against current AI capability.

## Key claims

- The rise of AI agents raises concerns about job displacement, diminished human agency, and overreliance on automation.
- The Human Agency Scale gives a shared language to quantify the preferred level of human involvement per task.
- WORKBank covers 1,500 domain workers and AI expert capability assessments across 844 tasks in 104 occupations.
- Worker desire and technological capability sort tasks into four zones, including an Automation "Green Light" Zone and a Automation "Red Light" Zone.
- AI agent integration is shifting core human competencies from information-focused skills toward interpersonal skills.

## Excerpts

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Brynjolfsson favours measurement before prescription, joining worker preference data with capability data rather than declaring a single automation verdict.
- The four-zone framework extends his augmentation-versus-automation distinction from the Turing Trap into a task-level, empirically gathered taxonomy.
- Naming a "Red Light Zone" where workers resist automation shows he treats worker agency as a design constraint, not an afterthought.
