# Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts about the Recent Employment Effects of Artificial Intelligence

## Citation

Brynjolfsson, Erik, Bharat Chandar, and Ruyu Chen. "Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts about the Recent Employment Effects of Artificial Intelligence". Stanford Digital Economy Lab, 13 November 2025. https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publications/canaries-in-the-coal-mine/.

## One-line summary

Using ADP payroll data, the authors document a 16% relative employment decline for early-career workers in AI-exposed occupations, concentrated where AI automates rather than augments labour.

## Key claims

- Early-career workers aged 22-25 in AI-exposed occupations saw 16% relative employment declines, while experienced workers stayed stable.
- Employment growth for young workers has been stagnant since late 2022, when generative AI tools proliferated.
- Declines concentrate in occupations where AI automates work, not in those where it augments work.
- The effect survives controls for firm-time shocks, with a 15 log-point relative decline for the most AI-exposed young workers.
- Adjustment shows up in employment rather than compensation, suggesting wage stickiness.
- Results hold after excluding technology firms and remotable occupations.

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- The paper sharpens the automation-versus-augmentation distinction from the Turing Trap into a measurable empirical test using real-time payroll data.
- Brynjolfsson again reads outcomes through heterogeneity, here by age and exposure rather than by skill, extending his distributional lens.
- The cautious framing, six facts rather than a single causal claim, reflects his preference for measurement over forecasting in a contested debate.
