# The Turing Trap: The Promise and Peril of Human-Like Artificial Intelligence

## Citation

Brynjolfsson, Erik. "The Turing Trap: The Promise and Peril of Human-Like Artificial Intelligence". Daedalus 151, no. 2 (2022): 272-287. https://doi.org/10.1162/daed_a_01915.

## One-line summary

Brynjolfsson argues that the pursuit of human-like AI that substitutes for workers, rather than AI that augments them, traps the economy in a low-power, high-inequality equilibrium he names the Turing Trap.

## Key claims

- The decisive economic question is whether AI substitutes for human labour or complements it.
- When AI substitutes for workers, those workers lose economic and political bargaining power.
- When AI augments human capabilities, humans and machines act as complements and create new value.
- The Turing Trap is a self-reinforcing equilibrium in which the powerless cannot improve their outcomes.
- The remedy is not slower technology but the removal of the excess incentives that favour automation over augmentation.

## Excerpts

> "When AI augments human capabilities, enabling people to do things they never could before, then humans and machines are complements."
> ~ The augmentation-automation distinction

> "As machines become better substitutes for human labor, workers lose economic and political bargaining power."
> ~ Abstract

> "Augmentation creates new capabilities and new products and services, ultimately generating far more value."
> ~ Abstract

> "The solution is not to slow down technology, but rather to eliminate or reverse the excess incentives for automation."
> ~ Concluding argument

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Naming the trap after the Turing Test signals his critique of human imitation as the wrong benchmark for AI design.
- The argument reframes technology policy as a choice over incentives rather than an inevitability, consistent with his stance that outcomes are designed, not fated.
- His coupling of economic and political bargaining power shows that he treats inequality as a governance risk, not only an efficiency question.
