# Minimum Wages and Rise of the Robots (2026)

## Citation

Brynjolfsson, E., Li, J. F., Miranda, J., Seamans, R. and Wang, A. J. "Minimum Wages and Rise of the Robots". NBER Working Paper 34895, February 2026. https://www.nber.org/papers/w34895.

## One-line summary

Higher minimum wages raise the likelihood that manufacturing plants adopt industrial robots, with a 10 percent rise linked to roughly 8 percent more adoption.

## Key claims

- Minimum wage increases raise robot adoption in manufacturing.
- Border discontinuities between states support the identification.
- A 10 percent minimum wage increase raises adoption by about 8 percent relative to the mean.

## Excerpts

> "This paper studies how minimum wage policy affects firms' adoption of automation technologies."
> ~ Abstract, NBER Working Paper 34895

> "Using both state-level measures of robot exposure and novel plant-level data on industrial robot imports linked to U.S. Census microdata from 1992 - 2021, we show that increases in minimum wages raise the likelihood of robot adoption in manufacturing."
> ~ Abstract (en dash in the original replaced with a hyphen)

> "Our preferred identification exploits discontinuities at state borders, comparing otherwise similar firms exposed to different wage floors."
> ~ Abstract, NBER Working Paper 34895

> "Across specifications, a 10 percent increase in the minimum wage increases robot adoption by roughly 8 percent relative to the mean."
> ~ Abstract, NBER Working Paper 34895

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- The paper treats automation as a response to factor prices, which suggests a price-theoretic view of technology adoption.
- Plant-level administrative data reads as a preference for causal identification over cross-country correlation.
