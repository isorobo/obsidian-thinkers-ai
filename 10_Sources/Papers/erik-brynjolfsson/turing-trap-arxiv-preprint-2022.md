# The Turing Trap: The Promise and Peril of Human-Like Artificial Intelligence (arXiv preprint)

## Citation

Brynjolfsson, Erik. "The Turing Trap: The Promise and Peril of Human-Like Artificial Intelligence". arXiv preprint arXiv:2201.04200, January 2022 (forthcoming in Daedalus, Spring 2022). https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04200.

## One-line summary

The preprint of the Turing Trap essay opens with Turing's imitation game and the cultural history of human-like machines, then argues that excess incentives push AI toward imitation rather than augmentation.

## Key claims

- Turing's imitation game made matching human intelligence the implicit goal of AI for decades.
- Human-like AI promises soaring productivity, leisure, and self-understanding.
- Many of the most powerful AI systems are not human-like, so over-focusing on imitation is a trap.
- When AI augments rather than mimics, humans retain power to claim a share of created value.
- Current incentives favour automation over augmentation among technologists, executives, and policymakers.

## Excerpts

> "In 1950, Alan Turing proposed an 'imitation game' as the ultimate test of whether a machine was intelligent."

> "There are currently excess incentives for automation rather than augmentation among technologists, business executives, and policy-makers."

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Grounding the argument in mythology and the history of robots shows his habit of framing technical economics in long historical arc.
- Defining human-like AI as one option among many, not the goal, restates his critique of the imitation benchmark.
- The preprint, posted openly before journal publication, reflects his practice of circulating ideas widely and early.
