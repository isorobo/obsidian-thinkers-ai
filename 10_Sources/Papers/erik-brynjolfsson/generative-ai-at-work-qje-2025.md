# Generative AI at Work (Quarterly Journal of Economics)

## Citation

Brynjolfsson, Erik, Danielle Li, and Lindsey Raymond. "Generative AI at Work". The Quarterly Journal of Economics 140, no. 2 (2025): 889-942. https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae044.

## One-line summary

The peer-reviewed version of the customer-support study reports a 15% average productivity gain, quality improvements for less-skilled workers, and small quality declines for the most skilled.

## Key claims

- Access to AI assistance increased issues resolved per hour by 15% on average, with substantial heterogeneity.
- Less experienced and lower-skilled workers improved both speed and quality of output.
- The most experienced and highest-skilled workers saw small speed gains and small quality declines.
- AI assistance facilitated worker learning and improved English fluency, especially for international agents.
- Gains were largest for moderately rare problems, where humans lacked experience but training data was adequate.
- AI assistance improved the experience of work; customers were more polite and less likely to escalate.

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- The published version adds a worker-learning and quality dimension, sharpening the augmentation case beyond raw speed.
- Noting small quality declines for top performers shows his willingness to report effects that complicate a simple optimistic story.
- The English-fluency finding for international agents extends his distributional lens to global labour.
