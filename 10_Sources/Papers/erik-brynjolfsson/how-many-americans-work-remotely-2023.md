# How Many Americans Work Remotely? A Survey of Surveys and Their Measurement Issues

## Citation

Brynjolfsson, Erik, John J. Horton, Christos Makridis, Alexandre Mas, Adam Ozimek, Daniel Rock, and Hong-Yi TuYe. "How Many Americans Work Remotely? A Survey of Surveys and Their Measurement Issues". National Bureau of Economic Research Working Paper No. 31193, April 2023, revised May 2023. https://www.nber.org/papers/w31193.

## One-line summary

A new nationally representative survey finds that about half the United States workforce worked remotely at least one day a week in late 2020, and that official statistics may undercount this by up to 25 percentage points.

## Key claims

- In October 2020, 31.6% of the continuously employed workforce always worked from home and 21.9% did so sometimes or rarely, totalling 53.5%.
- The Remote Life Survey addresses wide disagreement about the scale of the remote-work shift.
- Four survey-design factors explain measurement differences across sources.
- Excluding pre-pandemic remote workers explains most of the gap between the Current Population Survey and other measures.
- The BLS-CPS series may underestimate work-from-home levels by up to 25 percentage points.

## Excerpts

> "We find that in October 2020, 31.6 percent of the continuously employed workforce always worked from home (WFH) and 21.9 percent sometimes or rarely WFH, totaling 53.5 percent."
> ~ Abstract

> "Policymakers and researchers who turn to the BLS-CPS data series for an estimate of remote work prevalence in the American economy should note that it might be underestimating WFH levels by up to 25 percentage points."
> ~ Abstract

> "Our primary contribution is to document the wide dispersion in remote work measures and to explain how various survey decisions affect aggregate measurement differences."

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- The paper applies his measurement-first method to a new object, remote work, again exposing gaps in official statistics.
- Building a bespoke survey rather than relying on existing data reflects his willingness to construct his own instruments.
- Linking remote work to firm coordination and city structure shows his habit of tracing technology into organisational and spatial change.
