# Timelines and Takeoff Dynamics

A synthesis of thinker positions on AGI arrival timelines and the shape of capability expansion—from continuous scaling through discontinuous jumps to unknowability.

## 1. AGI Timeline Spectrum

### Near-term arrivals (2025–2030)

**Dario Amodei** [[20_People/dario-amodei/profile|dario-amodei]]: Powerful AI plausible by 2026 to 2027 under his Machines of Loving Grace definition: "country of geniuses in a data centre." Continuous progression followed by a compressed decade of transformative application. Avoids the label AGI; prefers capability-threshold framing. Key reference: [[10_Sources/Articles/dario-amodei/machines-of-loving-grace|Machines of Loving Grace]].

**Geoffrey Hinton** [[20_People/geoffrey-hinton/profile|geoffrey-hinton]]: Five to twenty years to AGI; shorter than he once believed. Revised downward from earlier estimates of thirty-plus years. Public x-risk advocacy from 2023. Key references: [[10_Sources/Media/geoffrey-hinton/60-minutes-2023|60 Minutes Interview (2023)]], [[10_Sources/Media/geoffrey-hinton/mit-tech-review-2023|MIT Technology Review (2023)]].

**Yoshua Bengio** [[20_People/yoshua-bengio/profile|yoshua-bengio]]: Short timelines; AGI plausible within five to twenty years. Lead author of the International AI Safety Report (2025). Advocates scientist AI over agentic AI as the safer path to advanced capability.

### Mid-term (2030–2050)

**Paul Christiano** [[20_People/paul-christiano/profile|paul-christiano]]: Median AGI by 2040; updated toward shorter from earlier estimates. Slow-then-fast trajectory; compute-governance matters. Key papers: [[10_Sources/Papers/paul-christiano/elk-2021|Eliciting Latent Knowledge (2021)]], [[10_Sources/Papers/paul-christiano/takeoff-speeds-2018|Takeoff Speeds (2018)]].

**Andrej Karpathy** [[20_People/andrej-karpathy/profile|andrej-karpathy]]: Useful agents arrive incrementally; AGI framing unhelpful. Treats the problem as decade-scale capability building rather than a binary arrival. Key talk: [[10_Sources/Media/andrej-karpathy/dwarkesh-podcast-agi-decade-away-2025|Andrej Karpathy: AGI is still a decade away (2025)]].

**Erik Brynjolfsson** [[20_People/erik-brynjolfsson/profile|erik-brynjolfsson]]: Transformational within the decade on task-by-task basis. Separates economic impact timelines from AGI definitions; productivity gains come incrementally as complementary investments mature.

### Long-term / sceptical (decades away, major caveats, or not AGI-path)

**Yann LeCun** [[20_People/yann-lecun/profile|yann-lecun]]: LLMs are not on the path to AGI; decades away. Real intelligence requires world models, energy-based planning, and objective-driven agents (JEPA). Timelines measured in decades; architectures need fundamental rework.

**Gary Marcus** [[20_People/gary-marcus/profile|gary-marcus]]: LLMs hit diminishing returns; AGI requires neurosymbolic breakthroughs. No imminent takeoff; bubble correction likely. Sceptical of lab insider timelines; treats transformational claims as marketing-inflected. Key book: [[10_Sources/Books/gary-marcus/taming-silicon-valley-2024|Taming Silicon Valley (2024)]].

**Daron Acemoglu** [[20_People/daron-acemoglu/profile|daron-acemoglu]]: Sceptical of near-term transformational claims. Treats AGI timelines as overstated by lab insiders. 2024 Nobel laureate in Economic Sciences; institutional economics lens.

## 2. Takeoff Dynamics

### Continuous scaling with gradual inflection

**Andrej Karpathy**: Gradual scaling with diminishing returns on current architectures. Treats capability growth as a long-tail problem.

**Erik Brynjolfsson**: J-curve; downward productivity at first as complementary investments lag, then rapid upward rise as organisations restructure. Emphasises task-by-task granularity over single-moment takeoff.

**Yann LeCun**: Continuous with architectural prerequisites. No fast takeoff; capability grows with compute and architectural iterations. Open-source decentralisation prevents concentrated breakthroughs.

### Continuous then rapid (compressed decade scenario)

**Dario Amodei**: Continuous progression followed by a compressed decade of transformative application. Rejects hard discontinuity. Argues for a period during which multiple labs sit at the frontier together. Period of increased control risk *because* no single lab dominates.

### Slow-then-fast with governance inflection

**Paul Christiano**: Slow-then-fast; compute-governance matters. Evaluations and compute-thresholds buy the time needed to solve alignment before explosive scaling. Key essay: [[10_Sources/Articles/paul-christiano/takeoff-speeds-2018|Takeoff Speeds (2018)]].

### Fast or uncontrollable once agentic

**Geoffrey Hinton**: Rapid and possibly uncontrollable once models acquire agency and sub-goal reasoning. Non-trivial probability of extinction-scale outcomes.

**Yoshua Bengio**: Risk of fast takeoff non-negligible; warrants precaution. Advocates scientist AI over agentic AI precisely because agentic systems carry uncontrollable-takeoff risk.

### No imminent takeoff / bubble-correction dynamics

**Gary Marcus**: No imminent takeoff; bubble correction likely. Current AI economics unsustainable; overinvestment risk. Views the market exuberance as misaligned to actual capability progress.

## 3. Evidence and Arguments

### Scaling-law arguments (continuous trajectory)

**Dario Amodei**: Empirical work on compute trends and scaling laws. [[10_Sources/Papers/dario-amodei/compute-trends-three-eras-machine-learning-2022|Compute Trends Across Three Eras of Machine Learning (2022)]], [[10_Sources/Papers/dario-amodei/scaling-laws-autoregressive-generative-modeling-2020|Scaling Laws for Autoregressive Generative Modeling (2020)]].

**Andrej Karpathy**: Deep-learning architecture progression and software engineering evolution. [[10_Sources/Articles/andrej-karpathy/software-2-0|Software 2.0 (2017)]], [[10_Sources/Articles/andrej-karpathy/vibe-coding-2025|Vibe Coding (2025)]].

### Architectural necessity arguments (decades remain)

**Yann LeCun**: World models and energy-based reasoning required before AGI. [[10_Sources/Papers/yann-lecun/path-towards-autonomous-machine-intelligence-2022|Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence (2022)]].

**Gary Marcus**: Neurosymbolic integration required. [[10_Sources/Papers/gary-marcus/deep-learning-a-critical-appraisal-2018|Deep Learning: A Critical Appraisal (2018)]].

### Economic observation (J-curve and complementarity)

**Erik Brynjolfsson**: Historical analogy to other GPTs (electricity, computers); productivity lags until organisation restructuring. [[10_Sources/Papers/erik-brynjolfsson/productivity-j-curve-2020|The Productivity J-Curve (2020)]], [[10_Sources/Papers/erik-brynjolfsson/generative-ai-at-work-2023|Generative AI at Work (2023)]].

### Governance and takeoff coupling

**Paul Christiano**: Compute-thresholds and evaluations can slow takeoff. [[10_Sources/Articles/paul-christiano/takeoff-speeds-2018|Takeoff Speeds (2018)]].

**Yoshua Bengio**: International coordination and precautionary governance can mitigate fast-takeoff risk. [[10_Sources/Papers/yoshua-bengio/international-ai-safety-report-2025|International AI Safety Report (2025)]].

## 4. Convergences and Tensions

### Convergences

**On the decade scale**: Dario Amodei, Geoffrey Hinton, Yoshua Bengio, and Paul Christiano all operate in the five-to-twenty-year or 2026–2027 / 2040–2050 windows. Even sceptics acknowledge *some* significant capability expansion within two decades.

**On task granularity**: Andrej Karpathy and Erik Brynjolfsson both separate "AGI" framing from task-by-task capability gains. The utility of AI comes before any binary AGI moment.

**On complementary investments**: Andrej Karpathy (education bottleneck), Erik Brynjolfsson (organisation restructuring), and Daron Acemoglu (labour institutions) all highlight non-technical constraints on impact.

### Core Tensions

**Takeoff shape—continuous vs. discontinuous**: Yann LeCun, Andrej Karpathy, and Erik Brynjolfsson see continuous scaling. Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio see material risk of fast takeoff. This divides on whether architectural breakthroughs are likely or whether agentic systems arise without explicit design.

**Path-dependence of timelines**: Dario Amodei, Geoffrey Hinton, and Paul Christiano are lab insiders with access to frontier models; their shorter timelines reflect what they observe. Yann LeCun, Gary Marcus, and Daron Acemoglu operate outside frontier labs; they are sceptical of insider claims. The disagreement is partly epistemological.

**Policy implications of timeline uncertainty**: Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio advocate strong precautionary measures *because* timelines are uncertain and fast takeoff is possible. Yann LeCun opposes restrictive regulation *because* timelines are long and open-source decentralisation is inevitable.

**Bubble-correction risk**: Gary Marcus predicts a correction in AI valuations and investment; no other thinker foregrounds this. Market-correction timelines could decouple from capability-arrival timelines.

---

## 5. The Unresolved Core Question

Thinkers converge that AI capability will expand significantly. They diverge on whether that expansion is *gradual* (continuous scaling, task-by-task, decades-long adaptation) or *punctuated* (capability thresholds cross in compressed windows, fast takeoff, loss-of-control dynamics materialise).

The disagreement is not empirical in the sense of "how many years" but *mechanistic*: Do current scaling laws hold? Do agentic systems emerge from language models or do they require architectural redesign? Can governance (compute-thresholds, evaluations, international coordination) slow takeoff?

**These questions remain open.**
